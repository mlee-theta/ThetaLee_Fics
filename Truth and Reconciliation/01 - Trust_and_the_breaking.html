Trust, and the breaking of

A week ago, Maddie Buckley had come home after leaving and getting the help she so desperately needed. Howie, her beautiful Howie, had been over the moon. He had followed her, she knew. He had spent weeks trying to find her, their precious Jee-Yun strapped in the car seat and along for the ride. He had found her, she knew, just like she knew the doctors had been a stone wall between them. They had told her, and they had respected her wishes.

Five days ago, there'd be the requisite Welcome Home party, and even though Maddie was better, she was still... Wary. Unsettled. But she had missed this, she had missed being with her friends, with her family. The depression had taken that from her, had twisted her thoughts and made her convinced that she was undeserving of them. Maddie had been so ashamed of herself, to the point that she had asked Howie not to tell anyone about her diagnosis. She regretted that decision, now. She regretted that decision much like she regretted every one that followed.

Five days ago, Maddie had noticed at the party how Howie and her brother... She didn't know how to phrase it, even in her thoughts. They interacted, they talked, they joked, but there was a stiffness to it all. Once, Chimney had raised a hand and she was certain she'd seen Evan flinch. She was certain of it, but she had brushed it aside because why on Earth would Evan flinch from Howie? Eddie had seen it, Maddie was also certain, because he had glared at her Howie. Not that Howie noticed, too caught up in having the love of his life back in his life.

It was yesterday, after Howie had left for work, that Eddie had come over. And she was grateful for him coming over, because even after weeks of help, she was still uncomfortable with being alone with Jee-Yun for too long, had plans to go visit Mrs. Lee in an hour's time. But this was odd, because wasn't he on the same shift as Howie? And then he'd said that he couldn't stay long, because Evan was watching Christopher for him while he ran an errand, and what the hell? Eddie not being on shift with Howie, okay, it happens. But Eddie and Evan?

"Buck won't tell you this," was how Eddie had started. He'd been holding Jee-Yun in his arms, smiling down at her adoringly. But then he'd sighed and set her down in her playpen and fixed Maddie with a stare that held the level of seriousness she'd expect to find on a call. "He won't tell you this, because he loves you, and he loves Chimney, and all he wants is you both to be happy. And, well, because he thinks he deserved it."

"Deserved what?" Maddie had asked

"Buck kept his promise," he'd said, instead of answering. Or, maybe, as part of his answer. And of course Evan had kept his promise, she'd made him pinky promise, and those were sacred. She regretted making him do it now, had regretted it then, but... But. "He'd kept it right up until Chimney got the hospital bill and confronted him for it. And... I get it, Chimney had been going insane with worry - not that I'm saying any of this is your fault! But I understand why Chimney was angry at Buck for keeping everything a secret. If there's anything I can understand, it's why someone else would be angry. Buck won't tell you what happened, but I will, because Buck is my best friend, and you're his sister, and you deserve to know."

"Eddie," Maddie had interrupted, "what happened?"

Eddie had sighed again, but he'd met her eyes all the same, and he'd said, "He punched Buck."

An hour ago, Howie had come home, tired and exhausted from a shift where he didn't quite know anyone. Maddie had asked for more, had wanted to know more, and Eddie had told her. In a moment of anger, Howie had punched Evan, bad enough that his face had been bruised for days and days. Howie had punched Evan, and when he'd come back from searching for her, he'd found his shifts rearranged. No one, with the exception of Hen, had wanted to work with someone who would punch a teammate and a friend in a moment of anger. But Howie hadn't told her this. He hadn't told her about punching Evan, either.

"Are you and Buck okay?" Maddie had asked when Howie came home.

"Of course we are!" Howie had lied to her. "Why would you ask that?"

"Because I know, Howie," she's said, and she could see him deflate.

"Buck told you?"

"Buck hasn't told me anything. Eddie did."

"Look, Maddie, I am so sorry," he'd said. "I was just... I was so worried about you, and then I found out Buck knew. I still can't believe Buck didn't tell me!"

"He didn't tell you because I made him promise not to," Maddie said. "Just like I made you promise not to tell him about Daniel. And you both kept those promises, promises I shouldn't have asked of either of you."

"I know, okay, I do. But I was just so angry when I found out."

"So you punched him?" Maddie asked, and Howie didn't meet her eyes, and she didn't know if she was more angry or heartbroken. "Is that what happened? You got angry with Buck and punched him?"

"Yes, okay? He knew, and he kept yammering on about how we didn't have to worry and you'd be back, and so I punched him!"

"And if the next time, it's me you're angry with?" Maddie asked, and she watched Howie's eyes widen and his jaw drop. But she had to ask, because it was a question that had been on repeat since Eddie had left the day before. She had to ask for the same reason Eddie had to tell her what happen. Because she did not claw her way out of her marriage with Doug, she did not bleed and kill, to end up back there again. "If the next time it's me you're angry with, what then?"

"Maddie, no, I would never-"

"Except that you would!" she shouted over him. "You did! Buck is my brother! He is my baby brother, and you hurt him! Because you were angry!" Maddie stopped suddenly, catching herself as her volume rose. Jee-Yun was down the hall, and she did not want to wake her up. "How am I supposed to trust you after this, Howie?"

Because that was the crux of it, wasn't it? Even if he hadn't punched her, he had still punched someone out of anger. And now? Now the 'what if' of it all would haunt them. What if this time, this argument, it was her walking into work with a black eye? Again. Maddie didn't believe he would do it, knew deep down that Howie loved her and would never harm her... But, once, she had believed and known that of Doug.

"How am I supposed to trust you now?" she repeated.

And Howie didn't answer, because he didn't have an answer.

"I'm packing an overnight bag, and Jee and I are going to Buck's." She should probably call ahead, would do so on the drive over. But if there was a truth she knew was cemented into the cosmos itself, it was that Evan would welcome her with open arms regardless of circumstance.

"Maddie, no, please-"

"No, Howie. I love you, but... I can't trust you, and I can't stay here if I can't trust you."

He was silent as she moved away him, silent as she gathered up clothes for a day or two, silent as she and Jee-Yun made for the door.

"I'm sorry," Howie finally said, just before the door closed behind her.

"I know," she said, because she did. She was certain he was sorry, even if neither were certain which part he was sorry for. "I love you."

"Text me when you get there?"

"I will," Maddie promised, and then let the door close behind her, and let the tears fall in earnest.
