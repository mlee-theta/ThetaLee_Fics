Fault

A week ago, Maddie had finally come home after months of being away, after months of getting the help she so desperately needed. She regretted many, many things surrounding the time leading up to and after her leaving, but she didn’t regret the leaving itself. She knew it hurt everyone, but it had seemed like the only option at the time, and honestly? Her leaving to get help was one of the better outcomes.

While she had been away, she had found an article online that had come out around the same time she had left. The article had spoken about a mother of two, a three year old and a one month old. She had taken her children in her arms and leapt out of a nineteenth floor window. Postpartum, the article had said. There but for the grace, Maddie had thought.

It had taken her so long to come to terms with how she didn’t have to feel guilty and ashamed for the way her mind was tormenting her. It had taken so long for her to understand that the way she was feeling was okay, that it wasn’t her fault, that no one would think less of her. Those thoughts and fears had led to her withdrawing from her support system, withdrawing from those she needed most. She knew that now, and while she could not undo the past, she could do better in the present.

Maddie had people in her life that would support her regardless of whatever her own mind tried to convince her of, and she was falling back on that support now.

Hours ago, Howie had come home from a shift where he didn’t quite know anyone. The consequences of the consequences of her decisions. Maddie had confronted him about the things Eddie Diaz had told her, and they had fought. Hours ago, Maddie had left again despite only just getting home, but this time she took Jee-Yun with her.

Now, she was in Evan’s loft, sat on his couch while she watched him coo over his niece. She had texted Howie that she’d gotten there safe, like she’d promised, and then turned her phone off. Evan had welcomed her in with open arms and a blinding smile, just like she knew he would. He hadn’t mentioned anything about the travel bag, or the dried tears, or the text out of the blue asking if she could spend the night. He wanted to, she could see that written plain on his face, but he had kept the questions to himself.

“She’s gotten so big, Mads,” he gushed, a bit of awe in his voice.

“Babies do that, Buck,” she told him, laughing.

“Well make her stop. Chris growing up so fast is bad enough, I don’t want Jee getting too big to be picked up too.” The comment, the easy equivalence between Chris and Jee, had her raising an eyebrow in amusement.

“I think we have a few years left before then,” she assured him, taking the high road instead of teasing him like she wanted. It hadn’t missed her notice that Taylor had been absent from the Welcome Home party everyone had thrown for her, that there was nothing in the loft around her that indicated the reporter’s presence in her brother’s life. It had definitely not escaped her notice that Eddie had stuck to Evan’s side the entire party, but that may have been because of Howie’s presence. Eddie had been wary of Howie being near her brother because Howie had punched her brother, and-

“I’m sorry,” she blurted out.

“Sorry for what?” Evan asked, turning to look at her, and Maddie found herself trying to see the bruise that had been left on his face. It had faded months ago, she knew, but she found herself raising a hand and tracing the spot where she imagined it to have been. Evan allowed her, unflinching and trusting, because it hadn’t been her touch that had hurt him.

“Eddie told me,” she said, and she watched his face scrunch up in confusion for a moment before it crumpled.

“Is that why you’re here?” he asked, hurriedly. “Mads, no, look, I’m okay! God, please don’t tell me this is why-”

“Evan,” she said, his name putting a stop to her brother’s verbal spiral. “He hurt you.”

“But I’m okay! We’re okay!”

“Are you? Because I saw you at the party, Buck. You flinched away from him, and I knew something was wrong then, but I didn’t know how wrong. And I am sorry that he hurt you.”

“Maddie, no, he was upset, but it wasn’t your fault.”

“Wasn’t it?” she asked. “He was upset because I left. He was upset because of the promise I had you make.”

“It still wasn’t your fault.”

“It wasn’t your fault, either,” Maddie said. “He had no right to hit you, no matter how angry he was about me leaving or about you not telling him. And I am so very sorry for putting you between us.”

“Mads, it’s okay.”

“No, it isn’t, because he hit someone he claimed to love while he was angry.” From the way Evan flinched, she knew he understood the implication of her words. “I know he isn’t like Doug, but I also know that I can’t stay in that apartment with him right now. Not when I can’t trust that the next time he’s angry he won’t hit someone else he claims to love.”

“Eddie shouldn’t have told you,” Evan said, hiding his face in Jee’s hair.

“He absolutely should have, Buck,” she corrected him. “I still love Howie, and I want to work through this. I just can’t be living with him for a bit while we do.”

“You know you can stay here however long you need,” he told her.

“I know,” she said. “Now give me my daughter back.”

“But I’m not done cuddling with-” he stopped, sniffing the air and making a face as he handed Jee-Yun to Maddie. “Absolutely, here you go.”

“Thanks. Uncle of the year.”

“I try.”

Later, after Jee had been changed and fed and changed again, after Evan had given her Dr. Copeland’s phone number and a strong recommendation of couple’s therapy, after a brief argument about sleeping arrangements that Maddie had won, she curled up on her brother’s couch and turned her phone back on. She sent off a quick text, almost immediately getting one back.

I love you.

i love you too.

im sorry.

I know.

Buck suggested couples therapy

anything.

Good night Howie

He sent a quick “good night” back and Maddie set her phone on the coffee table. She’d call Copeland in the morning, after Evan left for his shift, and then she’d take stock of everything afterwards. She had just come home again, and she wasn’t going to lose it now.

No matter how hard the task seemed at the moment.
