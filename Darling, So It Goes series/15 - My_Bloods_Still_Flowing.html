My Blood’s Still Flowing (My Heart Still Beats)

Eddie always hated working without Buck by his side. Not only did he not trust anyone to have his back as much as he trusted his husband, but he also didn’t operate nearly as seamlessly with the others as he did with Buck. They could all fill the space in a pinch whenever needed, especially Ravi, but he almost constantly had to remember to actually verbalize what he was thinking with them. But besides all of that, it simply, well. The job simply wasn’t as fun without Buck.

Work was work, without his best friend. The shifts dragged on just that bit more, even as he spent so much of his free time bombarding the phones of everyone at home, constantly checking to make sure that everything - that Buck - was still okay. The drive to and from the station was horribly silent, and the traffic even more unbearable than usual. 

It felt stupid to say that he was missing Buck despite the man not having actually gone anywhere, but that was the truth of the matter. From the moment Eddie left the house in the morning without him, he was missing the man.

And it was so very stupid, because he was also terrified of the day that Buck was cleared to return to work. Not that he had admitted as much to anyone except Dr. Copeland and, one night over hot chocolate while the rest of the family slept, his abuela. He would never, ever stand in Buck’s way, so he’d just have to deal with it. But still, it was there.

Eddie wondered, sometimes, if Buck had felt like this over those months he was out, his shoulder still healing. Not the missing him, he absolutely knew that Buck had missed him while he wasn’t working, but the fear.

Had Buck been afraid of him coming back to work? Had Buck been tempted, however momentarily, to bundle him up in cotton and never let him leave the house again?

“So.” Hen’s voice startled him slightly from where he was scowling down at his phone, waiting impatiently for someone to respond to the latest of his check-in texts. It was just him in the little den area of the fire station’s loft, or it had been until the paramedic’s arrival.

“So?” Eddie asked, looking up when she didn’t finish the thought. He was reclined on one of the couches, feet propped up on the coffee table in the exact way he would get after Buck for doing, back home.

“How’s it going?” she asked, sitting down in one of the arm chairs.

“I wouldn’t know,” he said, glaring back down at his phone. “No one’s texted me back yet. I mean, everything seemed fine when I last asked-” Twenty minutes ago. “-so it probably still is, but…”

“I wasn’t asking about at home, Eddie,” Hen said. “Or, maybe I was. But I meant with you. How is it going with you?”

Eddie blinked at her, not quite comprehending the question.

“I’m fine?” The statement came out sounding a bit more like a question than he meant it to, so he tried again. “I’m fine, Hen.”

“Uh-huh. And is that why you’re over here by yourself, sending your sixth round of texts asking how Buck was?”

Had it only been six? Well, that made sense, because they’d only been on shift for about three hours, and they’d been on a pair of calls for half that time, so- Wait.

“Firstly, I have not been that bad. And secondly, how would you even know?”

She held up her phone.

“Buck told me.”

“Ese pinche pendejo,” Eddie bit out. “He isn’t even supposed to be on his phone right now. It’ll mess with the Holter monitor.”

“Apparently he used the text-to-voice function, which made it so much easier to read than usual,” Hen said. “But we’ve swerved off-topic. I was asking about you.”

“And I said I’m fine,” he told her, furiously texting his husband to get off the phone, Buck . “Would I be better if Abuela responded faster, or even at all? Yes. But I’m fine.”

“I didn’t ask if you were fine,” his friend pointed out. “Because I knew you’d lie, like you just did. Just like I lied to Buck when he asked me the same thing, after Karen.”

Eddie heaved a sigh, dropping his phone on the couch beside him. 

“I am as fine as I can be, for someone whose husband was briefly murdered by God.”

“So not fine at all.” Perhaps it was rude to roll his eyes at her, but that didn’t stop him from doing it. She reached over and lightly smacked him on the head. “The main reason I came over is because Buck asked me to distract you. Apparently your grandmother’s started getting creative with what’ll happen to your phone, if you keep asking her how he is.”

He let out another sigh.

“Fine,” he said, and tried to ignore how petulant he sounded.

It was Hen’s turn to roll her eyes as she grabbed one of the game controllers off the table and tossed it at him.

“Y’know, I’m starting to think you and Ma might be hypocrites,” Buck said, grinning at the scowl that bloomed on Bobby’s face. They were in the Diazes’ backyard, watching Hoover run around in the grass while drinking glasses of iced tea. Bobby was holding Gavin, having pretty much stolen the toddler out of Buck’s arms the moment he arrived. “Seriously, I seem to recall getting in trouble last summer for doing… Pretty much exactly what you did.”

Just the other day, Bobby’s months-long amateur investigation into his sponsor, Wendell’s death had finally borne fruit. In the process, the rehab clinic burned down and he was nearly framed for arson.

“It wasn’t the same at all.”

“You mean you didn’t get a phone call from a young woman in trouble and then recklessly flew off the handle to save them by breaking into private property and physically assaulting a man?”

Bobby’s glare intensified, and Buck took an obnoxiously loud slurp of his iced tea.

“It’s not the same,” the captain insisted, and Buck nodded along gamely.

“Sure, sure. Absolutely different. For one, I was never arrested for what I did. You and Ma, on the other hand…”

“You’re enjoying this too much,” Bobby complained, smiling down at Gavin when he smacked at his chest for attention with a,

“Gwa’ps.”

“I think ‘grandpops’ is still a bit too much of a mouthful for him,” Buck commented. “Though he tries.”

“How did your appointment with the cardiologist go?” Bobby asked, carefully setting Gavin down when he started wiggling for freedom. The toddler balanced for a moment on his feet before falling onto his rear and then he was entranced by one of the large toy trucks he had gotten for his birthday, which had been left on the back patio at some point.

“It went,” Buck hedged. When the captain suddenly looked worried, he hurried to clarify. “I’m fine, the ticker’s still ticking. Everything’s good.”

“So all the extra tests, the Holter monitor…?”

“Just her doing her due diligence, apparently.”

“Good, good. Did she say when you’re cleared to come back?”

“As early as next week, if I wanted.”

Something must have shown on Buck’s face, or maybe his voice betrayed him, because Bobby was once again looking concerned.

“If you wanted?”

Buck dodged the question, and the paternal concern, by focusing on Gavin instead. He’d abandoned the truck and started crawling for Hoover, who’d come over and flopped down on the patio with them.

“Buck,” Bobby said, trying for his attention. “ If you wanted?”

“W- Well, she said ‘if you so choose,’ but I mean, it’s the same thing.”

“Do you want to start back next week?” the captain asked, and when Buck didn’t answer, “Kid, talk to me. Please.”

“I don’t know,” Buck confessed, still not meeting the other man’s eyes. “I have this constant sense of dread, and every time Maddie’s over, she looks at me like I’m going to die again. I’m having trouble sleeping. Falling asleep, staying asleep. And every morning I wake up and don’t see Eddie right away, I’m terrified I’m back in that dream.”

“Have you talked about this?”

“I talked to Copeland,” he said. “I- I haven’t said anything to Eddie about it. He’s struggling enough with his own side of things. Between the two of us, I’m not sure when the doc’s had time for any of her other patients.”

Bobby didn’t say anything right away. What he did do was push himself out of his chair and come to kneeling beside Buck’s, before reaching up and pulling him into a bone crushing hug.

“There’ll be a spot waiting for you,” he promised. “For when you’re ready, whenever that is. Next week or next month.”

“Thanks, Pops,” he said, and then because he couldn’t help himself, “Next month is next week.”

The captain let out a sigh so world weary, he half-expected it to anthropomorphize and start reminiscing.

“My grandson’s escaping.”

Buck pulled away enough to turn around, spotting Gavin crawling for the garden. Hoover was following along behind him.

“Ah, crap,” he said, leaping for his feet. “Gavin, no, don’t- Not Abuela’s hyacinths...”

The living room was quiet, lit only by Buck’s antique fire extinguisher-turned-lamp. The rest of the house was quiet, too, as Abuela and the kids were in bed and asleep. It was just Buck and Eddie, sitting on the couch and waiting for the other to speak first.

“I have no idea how to start this conversation,” Buck finally admitted.

“We can just… Jump right in?” Eddie suggested, grimacing as he did.

“This doesn’t seem like the kind of talk we just jump into, babe.”

“Honestly, I don’t want to have this talk at all.”

“I know,” Buck said. He didn’t want to talk about this, either. But they’d put it off for days and weeks, and he knew if they let themselves, they’d put it off until it was too late. “Neither do I. But I also don’t want to put you in the situation where… Where you’re having to make blind guesses.”

Like the situation they were very nearly in, not so long ago.

“You could just not get hurt again,” Eddie suggested, though it wasn’t very serious.

“C’mon,” Buck said, nudging him. “We got through that awful ‘who gets the boys if…’ conversation. I mean, we practically ranked our family and friends in order of who we trusted most. If we can do that, we can get through this.”

“I just… I don’t like the idea of deciding to- To let you die.”

“Eds.” Buck grabbed Eddie’s chin, made him look at him. “If the only thing keeping me alive is a machine, then I’m already dead.”

Eddie closed his eyes, like the act of not seeing would protect him from that truth.

“Okay,” he said, the one word trembling in the air. “Then we start there. Uh, I guess I pretty much feel the same…”
