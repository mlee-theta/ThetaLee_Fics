My Head's Underwater (I'm Breathing Fine)

Eddie did not enjoy therapy. He mostly just didn’t see the point of it, didn’t see the use in sitting around and talking about his problems. What good did it actually do?

Absolutely none, in his experience.

But he was here regardless, because his need for control had gotten out of control. The irony was not lost on him, even as he did his best to ignore it. Nearly killing a guy with his bare fists was not something he could just suck up and move on from. Nor could he simply ignore how Chris had felt like he needed to pretend that everything was okay, pretend he wasn’t hurting as well, like his emotions were a burden.

His kid could not be like him.

“We’ve talked a bit about the lead-up to your decision.” That’s how Frank referred to him joining an illegal underground street fighting ring. His decision. “But we haven’t actually talked about it. The lawsuit, I mean.”

“Why would we need to?” Eddie asked. “I mean, it wasn’t Buck’s smartest moment, but I understand why he did it. We talked about it on Halloween.”

“But it was the lawsuit that seemed to have been the capstone to everything.”

“Yeah, it made me angry, I’m not gonna lie,” he said. “We ran into him at the grocery store, and I kinda… Blew up at him. In front of everyone.”

There was shame, when he thought about it now. There had been shame then, but he’d tucked it away to be ignored. He should not have said those things to Buck, should not have screamed them in front of their friends and coworkers and total strangers.

He should not have weaponized his son like that.

(But it had worked, hadn’t it? He’d mentioned Chris and Buck had backed right down, just like-)

“Why?” Frank asked.

“Why, what?”

“Why were you so angry at Buck?”

“Because… Because I couldn’t reach out to him, because he didn’t stop to consider what it would do to us.”

Frank clearly thought that was an interesting answer, judging entirely by the way his eyebrows were raised.

“Us being?”

“Me and Chris and him.”

“Why?” Frank asked again. “Why did Buck need to consider you and your son in his decisions?”

“I… I don’t know,” Eddie confessed. “He just should have.”

Their session ended shortly after that.

Being scheduled to work on Christmas was upsetting, for everyone. It was especially upsetting for Chris, who had not taken the news well. Typically, he was happy and cheery and well-behaved. Eddie had certainly been blessed with him, a fact he bragged about often. But when he told his son that he wouldn’t be spending the holiday with him, well…

“It got ugly,” he told Hen. She had come over with Denny, a last-ditch attempt to make-up for what wasn’t going to come. “After I said I had to work on Christmas, he went straight to his room and wouldn’t speak to me for the rest of the night.”

And Eddie might have, possibly, called Buck afterwards. To complain, certainly not to pout or whine. Eddie did not pout or whine, especially about his son being upset with him.

“It’s just new,” the paramedic assured him. “He’ll get over it.”

“Abuela’s bringing him to my aunt’s,” he told her, wishing he had an ounce of her certainty. “I know he’s gonna have a great time. He just- Last Christmas was… Special. This one’s harder.”

Last year, all Chris had wanted for Christmas was his mother, and he had gotten that.

“Is that why you invited us all here?” she asked. “Trying to cram in as much Christmas cheer between now and then?”

“I thought it’d be nice to bring the boys together for a playdate,” he remarked, unable to fight the smile that took over his face whenever he looked into the living room. His coffee table was covered in gingerbread houses mid-construction, Chris and Denny in the process of putting theirs together with Buck’s cheerful assistance.

Buck being there looked right, it felt right. The other man hadn’t actually been invited, he just knew that Chris was upset and had come over to try and cheer him up. Him being there at the same time as Hen and Denny was mere coincidence.

“All three of them,” Hen quipped, laughing.

“Alright, who needs more cement?” Buck asked, happily stirring a cap of icing. Both boys shouted affirmations, and he sat down the cup to pick up what Eddie assumed was meant to be a wall. “Denny, we’ll do you first with this big ol’ piece.”

“Hey, Buck,” Chris piped up, quiet and somewhat hesitant.

“Yeah?”

“Can I spend Christmas with you?”

Buck looked to Eddie for help, and Eddie gave a small shake of his head. Of course Chris would want to spend Christmas with Buck if he couldn’t spend it with his dad. It only made sense.

(“Why were you so angry at Buck?”)

“I’m sorry, buddy,” Buck said, clearly torn up about having to turn the boy down. “I’m gonna be working on Christmas, with your dad.”

“Stupid work,” Chris said, and Eddie just gave Hen a look.

“I promise you,” she said. “He’ll forget he was even mad by New Year’s.”

“Is it just me,” Buck said, coming over to the dining table to join them. “Or does Christmas suck this year?”

Eddie picked up his cup of coffee and let out a sigh.

“It’s not just you,” he said.

“It’s been a rough year,” Hen said. “For everyone. So I’m happy to immerse myself in the magic of Christmas, and I just look forward to putting this year behind me.”

“I wouldn’t be surprised if there’s one more smack in the face to come,” Buck grumbled, and Eddie couldn’t help but agree.

As it turned out, Eddie got to spend Christmas with his son. As it turned out, everyone got to spend the day with their families. Because Buck had gone behind their backs to conspire with Athena and everyone else, set it up so they had their loved ones waiting with actual Christmas dinner.

Honestly, Eddie didn’t understand why he was so surprised. It was such an obvious Buck thing to do, to see a problem and then do his best to fix it. That’s what he had done when he introduced Eddie to Carla, after all.

Buck did grand gestures, and it was obvious how much he had hated having to disappoint Chris. He hated it just as much as Eddie did. So of course he would do his best to make up for it.

(“Why were you so angry at Buck?”)

When Eddie first saw Chris and Abuela waiting for him in the loft, he had been so overcome with joy at seeing them that he hadn’t noticed Buck hanging back. But afterwards, as the party went on, he noticed how much Buck did that. The man bounced between groups, from one family to the next, tormenting people with a sprig of fake mistletoe.

When he passed by them for the third - or was it fourth? - time, it was Abuela that he kissed. Just as it had been the last time. And Eddie wasn’t sure why it bothered him, that he was tormenting everyone else with the mistletoe - even Chimney - and not him. But it did. Bother him, that was.

What bothered Eddie more, though, was how Buck didn’t stick around with them. He swept through with a gift for Chris, and lingered long enough to watch him open it. But he didn’t stay. He didn’t stay with Bobby and Athena, either, or Maddie and Chimney, but it bothered Eddie much more that he didn’t stay with him and Chris and Abuela.

After all, Chris had wanted to spend Christmas with Buck, too. Just like he’d wanted to spend last Christmas with-

“Oh,” Eddie breathed out, the realization a smack to the face, and craned his neck to look for where Buck had gotten off to. He spotted him heading for the stairs, no one paying his departure any mind because he hadn’t been sticking with anyone else the entire time.

Eddie watched his back as he left, and could not help but be reminded of the sight of Shannon as she walked away, after she asked for a divorce.

Frank had asked, “Why were you so angry at Buck?”

And Eddie had answered, “Because he didn’t stop to consider what it would do to us.” And then he could not say why Buck needed to. Not then. But now?

When, exactly, had he started thinking of Buck in the same way he thought of Shannon? When, exactly, had Buck slotted himself into her role in his life? When, exactly, did he start thinking of Buck as more than just his best friend?

It happened so gradually, like the rising tide, that he simply hadn’t noticed until it was over his head and he was drowning in it.

That was how he and Shannon had started. They’d been friends first, then best friends. And then one day he looked at her and realized how beautiful she was and how much he wanted to kiss her.

(Honestly, Eddie should have recognized his feelings around the same time he caught himself appreciating the sight of Buck’s butt, because he had definitely only ever looked at Shannon in that way.)

Did Buck want to kiss him, though? Just because Eddie wanted to kiss Buck, didn’t mean that Buck wanted to kiss him. But, wait. That night in Buck’s kitchen, when he was telling Eddie about the woman Maddie had tried to help. After Buck - once again - apologized for everything, he’d started flirting, right?

“I’d still take you,” Buck had told him, smirking with his beer bottle right by his lips, and then he had sauntered over to him with his hand on his belt buckle, and Santa Maria, how did Eddie miss that? There was absolutely no heterosexual explanation for that.

And worse, Eddie had been flirting back. He just… Hadn’t realized it, at the time. But he had realized it now, and he had every intent to do something about it.

Making sure Chris was busy with Abuela and the others, Eddie took off down the loft stairs and after the man he so desperately wanted to kiss.
