The Calm Before

There are four fundamental states of matter that are observable in everyday life. The first three can be seen at any point of the water cycle: liquid, solid, gas. Water, ice, vapor. The fourth state, present all the time but regularly overlooked, is plasma. Plasma is seen all throughout the universe, twinkling in the night as stars and shining in the day as the sun. It is seen in neon signs, in the screens of the fancier and newer televisions, and on sci-fi shows and video games as high-tech weaponry.

And it is seen during every storm as lightning.

Lightning is the naturally occurring electrostatic discharge between two electrically charged regions that temporarily neutralize themselves. It could happen inside a cloud, between clouds, or from the clouds to the ground. It releases, on average, a gigajoule of energy instantaneously. 

For ages, people have seen lightning and associated it with gods. In the bible, thunder and lightning symbolized the divinity and power of God. Zeus, king of Olympus, wielded the thunderbolt in lieu of a crown. The same for his Roman counterpart, Jupiter. In the Americas, the sacred thunderbird created thunder by flapping its wings and lightning by flashing its eyes.

Lightning was powerful, and there used to be nothing that one could do against it. People respected that. In the middle of a storm, or on a clear and beautiful day, it could come down out of the sky and burn the world. It could fell trees, set fire to buildings.

It could smite an unlucky soul.

Bureaucracy could move fast, if there was proper motivation. Deidra, it turned out, had that proper motivation. 

Typically, family assessments for adoptions could take months. Buck knew this, had looked it up, and had been fully prepared to wait it out. He hadn’t been happy about it, but he’d been prepared for it. But Deidra, the social worker that had been working with Hen and Karen when they were fostering, took to the paperwork like a cattle driver in the Old West, herding everything along at a pace.

It helped, of course, that the background checks and such had already been done for Buck, back when he was adopting Chris as a step parent. By the end of the year, Eddie’s own had gone through, and a quick tour of their house had been done. References were called, and praises were sung.

By the end of January, Gavin had been moved from the Wilsons to Casa de Diaz. It almost took the jaws of life to pry him from Karen’s hands, and now they were hosting her and her wife for dinner far more often than they ever had before.

Also in January, Buck started at the LAFD’s Leadership Academy. One week a month, eight hour days. Stuck sitting in a classroom while someone lectured to him. Not to say that some of the things weren’t interesting, because they were, but… There was a reason he hadn’t done well in school. After the first day, he started recording the lectures on his phone so he could listen to them later. And sitting away from the others, in the back corner, so he didn’t annoy them too much when he inevitably had to move.

At least part of the day involved being broken up into groups to discuss and problem solve. That always played better to his strengths than sitting and listening did.

“Not to revive a dead horse, mi sol,” Eddie said one day, in early March. “But I think it’s time to consider Copeland’s idea of getting tested for ADHD.”

Chapter Summary

Lightning was weird and it did weird things. So weird that, until people started catching them on camera, science refused to believe that they were true. Like ball lightning, which was exactly what it sounded like. A ball of lightning that just hovered over the ground. There were Sprites, which looked like jellyfish, and Elves, which looked like rings or halos. Both of them appeared above the clouds, and got their names from acronyms.

There was dark lightning, too. Lightning without the light, it could produce gamma-ray flashes with the same amount of radiation as a full body CT scan. This lightning could blind satellite sensors hundreds of miles away.

And if lightning hit the ground, especially sand, it could create something called fulgurite.

Buck had seen it before, a consequence of living and working near beaches in both hemispheres. Arches of fused minerals jutting up, where lightning had struck during a storm. Forever frozen in the shape of its own blast. And beneath that, a system of root-like tubes. 

“Help!” the teenaged girl cried the moment she saw them getting out of the engine, running to them with her younger brother right behind. There was a still smoking beach umbrella where they’d been standing. “Help!”

“What happened?” Bobby asked, striding forward across the sand. They’d been called to one of the city’s many beaches, putting a pause to Chimney’s latest threats to maim - if not murder - his younger brother. It had been going on all shift, ever since Albert’s announcement that morning.

“We buried my dad,” the young girl answered. “And then lightning came out of nowhere!”

“It’s our fault!” the younger boy said as they picked up the pace, jogging behind their captain. “I shouldn’t have done it.”

Done what? Buck couldn’t help but wonder. Summon the lightning?

“It’s alright,” Bobby assured both kids. “We’ll get him out of there.”

“How? It got him in the neck.”

All they could see of the father was a head sticking out of the sand. His kids had not been lazy about their burial job.

“Was it the lightning?” Chimney asked as he set down his medical bag.

Something crunched beneath Buck’s foot, and he saw the fulgurite encircling their victim.

“The glass,” Buck answered. His eyes traced a line from the smoldering umbrella to the dad’s head.

“Sir, can you tell me your name?” Hen asked their vic as she and the Hans got to work, the others getting busy with digging him up.

“Ibrahim,” the man gasped out.

Buck tuned the paramedics out as he shoveled the sand. It was warm beneath his gloved hands. Not the unbearable heat of a billion volts, but the just-bearable heat of the Los Angeles sun. Which had been out all day.

Huh.

“No rain,” he commented, glancing up just to make sure. There were clouds, yeah, but also blue sky. “The hell did it come from?”

“I don’t know,” Eddie answered from beside him. “But I really hope it doesn’t strike twice.”

He shifted to give himself a bit more space from the nearest of them, and hunched down a bit as he continued digging.

“Why is this happening?” the boy asked, sounding increasingly upset. “I’m so sorry, Daddy!”

“It’s okay, Omar,” the dad said. “I’m okay.”

It might have been more convincing if he could put more life into the words. Or if he wasn’t currently buried. Or if the un-fused sand around him wasn’t wet with his blood from the jagged edges of the glass cutting into his neck.

He held up a piece of the glass he broke away while digging out their father.

“That’s what happened to your dad.”

“Let’s get a backboard over here!” Bobby called out, and Buck turned his head to see that they’d made fast work of the sand burying Ibrahim. Chimney was getting an IV ready, while Hen and Albert were wrapping gauze around Ibrahim’s neck.

“I get that, wanting to fix everything.” Boy howdy, did he. Copeland once spent two entire sessions picking at that. “But sometimes things just come out of nowhere that we don’t expect.”

“He means lightning,” Rasha explained for her brother’s sake. Buck hadn’t necessarily meant lightning specifically, but he hadn’t not meant it, either.

“You haven’t changed that much,” Daniel interrupted to say. “You’ve barely changed at all. There’s a reason the brass decided you weren’t good enough to play captain while Bobby was gone. There’s a reason Chimney and Hen were both so ready and willing to believe you’d whore yourself out behind your husband’s back, why Eddie was willing to believe it.”

“No,” Buck said, shaking his head. “Eddie and I, we talked about that. We’ve gone over all the reasons, all the ways we both screwed up.”

“But nothing to say about Hen and Chimney?”

“Hen apologized, Chimney apologized, they both-”

“Immediately assumed the worst of you,” Daniel said. “Because you haven’t really changed, and they can see that.”

“They apologized.”

“But it doesn’t change anything, does it?” The other man shook his head. “Why are you in such a hurry to wake up?”

“Because if I don’t, I’m pretty sure I’ll die,” Buck said. “And I promised I wouldn’t leave them. I don’t want to leave them.”

“Why not? They’re going to leave you.” Daniel said it like it was a simple point of fact. The sky was blue; the sun was bright; they were going to leave Buck. “Wouldn’t it be better to be the one to leave first, this time?”

“What? No, no.” It was Buck’s turn to shake his head. “They’re not going to leave me.”

“Again.”

“What?”

“You meant to say that they’re not going to leave you again. Except you can’t really say that, can you? You have absolutely no guarantee that when you wake up, when everyone sees how hurt you are - again! - that they won’t just leave you behind. Just like they did last time.”

“That isn’t going to happen this time.”

“How can you be so sure?” Daniel demanded. “How can you possibly be so certain that they’ll stick around? That history won’t repeat itself?”

“Maybe I can’t be,” Buck said. “But I trust them. I trust that Pops won’t pull the same bullshit again, and that if he does, I trust he’ll listen to me when I yell at him for it. I trust that Ma will keep visiting, that Hen and Chimney and Albert and Ravi won’t just forget about me when I’m not there. I trust that Maddie will keep coming back, and that Eddie will keep having my back. I trust that when I go home - and I am going home - that it won’t be empty and cold. That it’ll be full and warm.”

“Why? Because they’re your family?”

“Yes!”

“Why would anyone want a family with you?”

Buck couldn’t help it. He burst out laughing, and laughed even harder at the frustrated look on Daniel’s face.

“I’m- I’m sorry-” He shook his head. “Actually, I’m not sorry at all. Because this, all of this, is ridiculous. I have a family. Maybe I wake up in the mornings still and find myself wondering how I ever got so lucky, maybe I fall asleep afraid that the other shoe will drop. But it doesn’t change the fact that I have a family. I am surrounded by people who love and cherish me in ways I never thought I would ever have. A figment of my imagination giving voice to all of my insecurities isn’t going to change that.”

“They don’t care about you,” Daniel spat. His brother was looming over him, getting right in his space, but Buck didn’t flinch away. “Why would they? You’re not good enough, you never have been. The only reason you were ever born was for spare parts, and they were defective. You’re defective. Broken. An utter disappointment and a waste of-”

The fist that smashed into Daniel’s face seemed to come out of nowhere. It sent the man sprawling onto the floor, knocked out cold.

“No one,” Eddie snarled, standing at Buck’s side, “talks about my husband like that.”

“There you are,” Buck said, leaning into his side. “I have been looking all over for you.”

“I’m not really here,” the man reminded him, wrapping an arm around him.

“I know,” he said, because he did. This was all a screwed up dream, after all. “I want to go home.”

“Then let’s go home.”

Eddie took his hand, the one holding the thread. The drumbeat doubled. Doubled as in sped-up, in the way Buck’s heartbeat always did when Eddie took his hand, even after all this time. But also doubled as the one beat was joined by another, the two rhythms not-quite synced.

They stepped over the unmoving form of Daniel and made their way to that so familiar door. Eddie took the hand he was holding and placed it on the handle, right over the knotted thread. Buck gave a gentle push and the door swung open, as easy as it ever did.

He took a step inside.

He took a breath.

The chair in Buck’s new room was much more comfortable than the one in the ICU, in Eddie’s professional opinion. They’d moved him when he started breathing on his own and kept on doing so. The change in wards also came with a change of rules, looser and more relaxed than in the ICU. For example, they hadn’t had to sneak Chris in again like they were on some secret spy infiltration mission.

At the moment, Eddie was alone in Buck’s room, except for a sleeping Gavin in his lap. He was reclined in the much more comfortable chair beside Buck’s bed, facing away from the sun shining through the window. His eyes were focused on Buck, on the rise and fall of the man’s chest.

It was why he noticed it the moment the man started waking up.

Eddie was immediately on his feet, adjusting a grumbling child in his arms, grinning widely when he finally spotted those pretty blues for the first time in what felt like forever. The grin fell when Buck immediately started tearing up.

“Hey, hey, mi sol, no,” he said, leaning in and wiping them away with his free hand. “What’s this for?”

“I had the worst dream,” Buck croaked.

“Oh. You- You had a bad dream?” Eddie couldn’t stop the laughter, even when Buck pouted at him for it. “I am so sorry that you had a bad dream.”

“You should be,” Buck said, sniffing a bit. “You weren’t there.”

“I’m here now,” he told him, sitting down on the edge of the mattress, adjusting Gavin so he wasn’t quite lying between them. “And so’s this little guy.”

Gavin, still waking up from the sudden motion, babbled sleepily.

“Where’s everyone else?” Buck asked.

“Chris complained about being hungry,” Eddie explained. “And you were taking your sweet time waking up, so they all went down to the cafeteria. I should call the nurses and let them know you’re awake. And I definitely need to tell Chris, I promised him I would.”

“You should definitely get on that, then,” he said, and then, “I love you.”

“I love you, too.”

“And I’m okay.”

“You’re okay now.”

“...did I get any cool scars?”

Eddie could not contain the eyeroll brought out by the question. He juggled Gavin a little bit as he grabbed the remote attached to the bed, hitting the Call button. A nurse, he knew, would be ambling by in due time.

“Your father’s a tonto,” he stage-whispered to Gavin, and smiled at the sound of Buck’s tired squawk of offense.

Where the Heart Is

Chapter Summary

In which Buck is awake, and more than ready to be home.

Buck did not like being stuck in the hospital. Again. It was fine the first day he was awake, or at least the first few hours of the first day. But it got very old, very quickly. He just wanted to go home, damn it. To sleep in his own bed, to eat the delicious food that Abuela was bound to force on him. Well, her food and also a pizza or three.

Sure, okay, he understood why he was currently imprisoned. He got struck by lightning, he died for a bit there, and then spent a lot of time unable to breathe on his own. He understood all of that. He just didn’t like the fact that he couldn’t go home the moment he was awake.

On the bright side, he was very rarely left to stew alone. There was almost always someone with him. A near constant Diaz presence at his bedside, usually more than one Diaz at a time, that was supplemented by the others. It was to the point that Buck was getting suspicious that there might have been an actual schedule worked out for it.

“They’ve agreed to let you make up for the last half of the academy in the summer,” Bobby announced, during one of the rare times that there was no Diaz present except Buck himself. Eddie was gone to pick Chris up from school - the kid had (very reluctantly) started going back, now that Buck was in the clear - which was how they’d gotten onto the topic of the Leadership Academy at all.

“That’s cool of them,” Buck said, focusing on the cards in his hand. They were playing Gin on the rolling tray table that his meals were usually placed on, and he was losing. Which was pretty much the norm. 

“Speaking of school, though,” Bobby said, smiling at the card he just drew. Buck did not like that smile, it was far too satisfied. “You were a teacher?”

“In my dream?” He asked, making sure. The captain nodded. “Yeah, apparently.”

“What subject?”

“Hell if I know. Never made it in the classroom, which tracks.”

“You know, I can see it.” Bobby, when Buck looked up from his cards, was staring at him intently. “You being a teacher. I think you’d be good at it.”

“Really?” Buck scoffed. “Being back in class has reminded me of how awful of a student I am.”

“Doesn’t mean you wouldn’t be a good teacher,” the captain countered. “But what subject? Definitely not anything math related.”

“Obviously.”

“I’m thinking…” Bobby paused for effect. “Drama.”

“Because I’m dramatic, right?” Buck rolled his eyes. “Ha ha ha.”

“If not that, then something science or social studies.”

“My parents are teachers,” Buck told him, nodding at the man’s look of surprise. “Yeah, I know. I think it’s how they met? But it never made sense to me, either, what with how they handled, y’know. Me.”

“No one’s ever said a teacher has to be good with children.”

“That’s true,” Buck agreed, nodding. “You and Ma helped me, y’know. In my dream. Even when I couldn’t really tell you what was wrong, when I could barely explain it myself, you both just… Helped.”

“Of course we did,” Bobby said, because, well. Of course they did. There had never been a doubt that they would, not even in that nightmare world. The captain placed his cards on the table, face-up, announcing, “Gin.”

Buck threw his cards at the man.

“So do you know how long you’ll be out for?” Albert asked. It was him and Chimney visiting, this time.

“Becker said a few weeks,” Eddie answered for Buck, raising his head from where he peered over Chris’ shoulder at his homework questions. It was Math at the moment, so the kid was definitely not going to be asking for their help. “No strenuous activity, no heavy lifting.”

“How long is a ‘few,’ exactly?” Chimney asked, and Buck shrugged.

“Long enough for my ribs to heal, at least,” he said, and then hurried to grab Gavin, the movement jarring his aforementioned ribs a little. The boy was on the bed with him, and was now making a lunge for the edge. “Hold up, little guy. Gravity hurts.”

“Want me to take him?” Eddie asked, having clearly noticed Buck’s wince.

“Not on your life.”

“So she was warned, right?” Chimney asked. “His doctor, I mean. She was warned that the last time he was supposed to rest easy and give himself time to heal, that… Well.”

“That time,” Buck said, pointing a finger at time with his free hand. His other was keeping a firm hold on Gavin’s ankle. “Was not my fault.”

He might have been a little bit overeager to return to work, but the blood clots had absolutely not been his fault. The blame laid squarely on how his body reacted to the screws helping keep his leg bone together. His doctor had said so, damn it.

“She was warned,” Eddie told him.

“Apparently just about everyone warned her,” Buck said, rolling his eyes. “I am not that bad.”

“Yes you are.” Chris didn’t even look up from his homework, which somehow made the betrayal worse.

“Someone,” Buck said, mock glaring at him, “wants double vegetables for dinner.”

“Then it’s a good thing ‘Buela’s the one making dinner tonight.”

“That sass on you, kid.”

“I wonder where he gets it from,” Eddie drawled.

“You,” was Buck’s instant answer.

Any retort from Eddie was interrupted by Albert’s phone going off. It was sitting on the rolling tray table, and although Buck couldn’t see the screen, Chimney could. And Buck could see Chimney’s scowl.

“I’m not going to tell you how to live your life-”

“Yes you are,” Albert scoffed, picking up his phone.

“But I am going to judge you for it,” Chimney continued, as if his younger brother hadn’t said anything. “You’re still talking to the bas- the man? After everything he said to you?”

“Who?” Buck wondered.

“Our father! And what’s worse, he has him as ‘Daddy’ on his phone! I mean, Al, you are an adult, a grown-”

Albert, staring him dead in the eye, cut him off by answering the call with a,

“Hey, Rav.”

The look of utter horror on Chimney’s face was enough to send Buck into peels of laughter, which was not kind to his ribs.

“I don’t get it,” Chris said, blinking at them.

“And I hope you never will,” Eddie told him, patting the kid on the head, which had Chris ducking and scowling at him.

“Please, God,” Chimney whispered as Albert stepped out of the room to talk to Ravi, “let me be struck by lightning next. I’ve learned too much.”

“So you’re not dead. This is a shocking turn of events, sunshine.”

“You say the sweetest things to me, Teek.”

“Yeah, yeah. You love me.”

“No, I love Eddie. I like Carlos, and I tolerate you.”

“Rude. Extremely rude. I light up your life.”

“...sure.”

“Whatever man. Listen, I’m going to need you to try and not nearly die any more. Because if I have to listen to Eddie sounding like that over the phone again, or in person, I will take a flying leap off the wagon.”

“What the hell.”

“I am talking the most glorious, beautiful, ten-out-of-ten Olympic swan dive anyone has ever seen. Straight into a pool of alcohol and pills. Do you want to be responsible for that, Buck? Do you?”

“Holy shit, Teek.”

“Do you?”

“No, of course not.”

“Damn right. So no more being struck by lightning. Or trying to die some other way.”

“I’ll try, promise.”

“Good.”

“Now if you’re done being melodramatic-”

“Hey!”

“Catch me up on everything that’s been happening down there. How did Paul’s date go? Is there anything new with the wedding planning?”

“Apparently his date went awkwardly. She spent the entire time going down memory lane. And considering she knew him before, y’know.”

“Oh, geeze.”

“Yeah. And the wedding planning … Well, Dad and Carlos’ mom joined forces in an unholy alliance to ruin everything good in my life….”

“I would really like it,” Taylor Kelly said, when it was her turn to visit. Or, Buck thought it was her turn. He was absolutely convinced that there was an actual schedule now, though the reporter had shown up while Hen was there. Maybe the two got their times confused? It led to somewhat of an awkward atmosphere until the paramedic left, Eddie walking her out. “If you did your best to not get hurt like that again.”

“I have already been threatened multiple times,” Buck assured her. Admittedly, half of those times had been from TK. “I promise, I have absolutely zero plans to repeat this experience.”

“I’m serious, Buck,” the reporter said. “You are pretty much my only friend. It would suck if you were gone.”

“Wait.” That didn’t sound right. “I can’t be your only friend. There’s… You have more than that. More than just me. I mean, you talk to Josh. Don’t you?”

“To gossip and to bitch,” Taylor said. “Apparently not to tell me that our mutual friend has been struck by lightning and is in the ICU.”

Oh. Wait.

“But someone did tell you, right?”

“One of the others at the station heard it through the grapevine and told me,” she said, leaning back in her chair, looking for all the world like she didn’t care about what she was talking about. “I swung by your place and Eddie told me which hospital to go to.”

“But that doesn’t mean you don’t have other friends…” Buck tried.

“I’m Taylor Kelly, the Bitch,” she said, and it was a phrase that Buck had heard countless times, often from Athena and the others. To the point that it almost felt like her full name. “I am what I am, it is what it is. Not all of us are like you, needy and completely unable to stand it when someone doesn’t like them.”

That might have been the worst lie she had ever told, Buck couldn’t help but think.

“I’ll make sure they know to tell you, next time,” he said, instead of calling her out on it.

Taylor rolled her eyes.

“There shouldn’t be a next time!”

“Well it’s not like I threw myself in front of the lightning, Tay…”

“I can walk to the exit,” Buck insisted, glaring at the wheelchair the nurse had brought.

“It’s hospital policy, mi sol,” Eddie reminded him, waiting patiently for him to move from the bed to the chair. “Do you need me to help you?”

“No,” he said, pouting a little as he shoved himself out of the bed and took the single step to the wheelchair. “Let’s go.”

Eddie practically oozed self-satisfaction as he pushed the wheelchair out. Buck pouted and bitched the entire time, because he could walk himself out, damn it. Not that either Eddie or the nurse walking with them seemed to care.

Eddie’s truck was parked right outside the front entrance, and Buck glared at the nurse when they stepped up and acted as if they were going to help him in. His husband, at least, had the sense to step back and let him do it himself. Even if it took far more energy than it should, and his ribs protested, and gripping the handle to pull himself up irritated the still healing skin on his palm.

(The gauze had been removed, revealing that he was probably going to get some new scars out of it. He was still disappointed that the Lichtenberg figure faded before he woke up, though.)

The drive home was quiet, comfortably so, and slowed by the midday traffic. Buck was going home, which is what he had been wanting ever since he opened his eyes and had his dreamed up dead brother staring down at him. It was quiet and it was comfortable and he had to ruin it.

“We need to talk,” Buck said, curled up on his side so he could watch his husband.

Eddie sighed, letting go of the steering wheel so he could reach over and take his hand.

“Yeah,” he agreed. A reluctant agreement. “We do.”

“Not now, though. Later.”

“Yeah.”

Buck had gotten the story of what had happened in the waking world through bits and pieces. He knew he’d been in limbo for a time, there. That they had nearly entered uncharted waters, that Eddie had nearly been in a position that they hadn’t accounted for. If Buck hadn’t started breathing on his own, if it turned out that he was unable to survive without a machine… They hadn’t talked about that scenario, about what either of them wanted in that case.

It was not going to be a pleasant conversation, so Buck had no problem putting it off indefinitely.

The street in front of their home was crowded, although there was a space left in the driveway for the truck. Athena and May were both sitting on Abuela’s glider on the porch, though they both stood up when the truck pulled in, May practically sprinting to the passenger side.

“You saw me at the hospital yesterday,” Buck reminded her, laughing as she dragged him out and into a hug.

“That was yesterday,” she said, squeezing him extra hard before letting go.

“Were you waiting for us?” Eddie asked, getting out as well.

“Giving time for the Claritin to kick in,” Athena answered, and both Eddie and Buck nodded in understanding. A stash of the allergy meds were kept at both the Grant-Nash house and Casa de Diaz for the women, because proximity to Hoover and his fur had them both sneezing their heads off otherwise.

“How crazy are the decorations?” Buck asked, allowing Eddie to guide him up the porch steps with an arm around his waist.

“There are some balloons,” May answered, and from her tone and expression, Buck gathered that “some” translated to “a lot.” “I think they were every single lightning bolt shaped one Albert could find. And there’s a pinata in the shape of that red car from Cars.”

“Excellent.” He knew he could depend on the younger Han for these things. “So I’m thinking I should make Pops cry today.”

“You’re gonna tell him?” Eddie asked, pausing in the action of opening the door.

“I’m gonna tell him.”

They ignored the curious looks May and Athena were giving him as Eddie threw the door open. There was a shout from the small crowd inside, and Buck allowed himself to be dragged into hug after hug, despite the fact that he had literally seen each person at least once since waking up. Eventually, mostly through Eddie’s insistence, Buck made it to the couch and sank into the cushions. Chris was quick to sit beside him, pressed into his side.

“Where’s my little guy?” Buck demanded, after giving Chris a tight squeeze. Maddie, the one holding her nephew hostage, was clearly reluctant to give Gavin up to him. “You have your own kid, Mads.”

“You’re just as bad with Jee,” she pointed out, handing him over.

“Shaddup,” he said, because she wasn’t wrong, and then cleared his throat. “Pops?”

“Yeah, kiddo?” Bobby took a step closer, obviously prepared in case he needed to go fetch something for him.

“Eddie and I,” Buck said, glancing at his husband, “would like to properly introduce you to, uh. To Gavin Robert Diaz.”

He held Gavin up a little, so that he was standing on Buck’s thighs, his tiny arms flapping in the air. The fire captain blinked at them both.

“Robert?” he asked, his voice as delicate as smoke.

“Robert,” Buck confirmed with a nod. “I- I mean, not legally yet, since we can’t put it on the birth certificate for a few months. But, uh, but yeah. Gavin Robert.”

Buck had been right days ago, with his prediction of Bobby’s reaction to the news. And he’d been right about his reaction to the man’s reaction.

At least there was plenty of food to make up for all the crying.
