It’s Too Late to Say You’re Sorry (Say You’re Sorry, Still)

Three months, and Chimney was no closer to finding Maddie. Three months of long drives, of cheap hotels, of constant stress. He had driven to Oxnard, first, as that had been where Maddie withdrew the money. But after that? He hadn’t an actual lead to follow, although that hadn’t stopped him. He simply started driving eastward, away from the coast, under the assumption that she wouldn’t have gone north to cold climates, or south into a foreign country. Honestly, he’d been relieved when Buck had finally, finally left him a voicemail that wasn’t just him whining. The fact that the man had given him an actual lead - Boston - was all the better.

That Maddie had called Buck, rather than him, had hurt. But that was okay, he’d get to Boston and he’d see her in person and tell her how wonderful she was and how much he missed her. He would help her realize how great of a mother she was, and how she shouldn’t have run away like she had, not when she had him and Jee. And then they’d go home to Los Angeles with Jee and the Lees and everything would be okay. It had to be.

It had to be okay.

But Boston was not a small city, and even worse, the bells that Buck had heard - the Old North Church bells, supposedly - had replicas back in Los Angeles. And as he told Hen, if he drove all the way to Boston for nothing, he was going to kill the man. The only productive thing to come out of it was meeting Eli again, but that wasn’t enough to make up for the utter dead end he had run into.

Eli had helped him realize that maybe, just maybe, he was going about things the wrong way. But he wasn’t going to give up. He’d take a breather, like his old friend had suggested, but he was going to keep looking.

“First new message…”

“Hey Chimney, did you know Albert got paramedic certified? It was a surprise to me, too. He’s with us now, partnering up with Hen for the moment. I’m guessing-”

“Message deleted.”

“You got paramedic certified?”

“Yup. I figured it would come in handy, and it did!”

“Come in handy for you to replace me, you mean?” Chimney snarked.

“What the hell?” Albert asked, sounding taken aback. “No one is replacing anybody.”

“You’re in my spot at my station with my friends,” he sneered. “Sounds like replacing to me.”

“The only friend you have here right now besides me is Hen,” his brother told him. “Maybe Buck-”

“Buck’s just whining, ignore him.”

“Yeah, that wasn’t-” The words cut off with a sigh. “I’m gonna go, I promised Ravi I’d hang out with him after work today. Bye.”

“You’re friends with Probie?” Chimney tried to ask, but the line went dead before the words had really left his mouth.

Hershey had been a bust, was always going to be a bust, but Chimney had to try. He hadn’t particularly wanted to visit Margaret and Phillip, and he didn’t particularly believe that Maddie would go to them, but he didn’t really have anything else. Besides, it hadn’t been that far away from where he’d pretty much set up base in Boston. The pair of them hadn’t heard from her any more recently than he had, though, and had no idea where else she would have gone. They were even less helpful than her brother.

“I don’t know what to tell you, Howard,” Phillip had told him. “But I’m honestly not surprised.”

“What? Why?”

“She’s always made poor choices, and then ran from them,” Margaret explained. “She and Evan, both. We had hoped that at least she had grown out of it. We’d thought she’d come to her senses with you, but I guess she’s still being difficult, even with a child of her own.”

“Some people,” Phillip said, putting a hand on his wife’s shoulder and giving her a blinding smile that made him think of Buck, “just aren’t meant to be parents.”

There wasn’t even an ounce of irony to his tone.

Chimney hadn’t stuck around for dinner, and went back to Boston the next day.

“You’ve reached Maddie’s voicemail. Leave a message!”

“Hey Maddie, I really miss you. Jee misses you. I just wish you would call me, tell me where you are. Let us come get you. We’re back in Boston. Your parents were of absolutely no help, but I don’t know why I thought otherwise. I must have gone insane, I guess. Jee wishes you a Happy Thanksgiving, by the way.”

“Baby Han isn’t that annoying to be partnered with, actually,” Hen told him. “Much better than Eddie, let me tell you. I found myself missing the days that man kept everything to himself.”

“Baby Han?” Chimney asked.

“It’s what everyone’s been calling him, after Buck-”

“Oh. Buck,” he spat the name out like a glob of bile.

“Are you still- Seriously, Chim?” Hen’s sigh was loud and made the line crackle. “Let it go, you’ve had weeks now. You need to get over this before you come back, or else you won’t be able to come back.”

“It’ll be fine, Hen,” he assured her. “I’ll find Maddie, and when I come back, everything will be back to normal.”

“Yeah, I doubt that,” she said. “Because you set fire to pretty much all your bridges over here.”

“Bobby will give me my spot back, and then Albert can go back to his crew. You all need me. I’m the heart of the one-eighteen, remember?”

“Chimney, I hate to break it to you,” she said. “But I don’t think that’s remotely true anymore.”

“First new message…”

“Listen, I get that you’re angry with yourself for everything that happened and so you’re taking it out on me, but it’s been months and- Whatever. I have something happening on Christmas, and I would really like for Jee to be there. And you. I asked M-”

“Message deleted.”

“You’ve reached Maddie’s voicemail. Leave a message!”

“Hey honey, it’s officially past midnight, so merry Christmas. I have a present for you, for when I see you. I know I’ll see you soon. Tomorrow, I’ll take lots of pictures of Jee’s first Christmas, so you’ll get to enjoy it with us, even if you're not here. I promise. Love you.”

Christmas morning, and most of the afternoon, was spent watching old claymation movies on the hotel television with Jee. It was a lonely holiday, with Chimney hoping that his phone would ring with a call from Maddie, finally. That the season for giving would give him a miracle, however small. It didn’t, though. It chimed with texts from Albert and Hen, wishing him a merry Christmas, but the only one who called was Anne Lee.

Well, Anne and Buck. The latter had tried calling him the night before, and then again in the morning, but he’d sent it directly to the mailbox both times. Chimney hadn’t been in any mood to listen to the man’s excuses or to hear him begging for forgiveness or whatever it was going to be this time. Unless it had something to do with Maddie, there was nothing the man could say that he cared about. 

Oddly, he hadn’t left any messages either time. Perhaps he was actually learning.

Around 3:30, Chimney called Albert, or at least he tried: it rang and rang, but his brother had never picked up. When he checked his instagram, he saw why. He was out somewhere with Ravi, apparently on a date, going by their clothing. It had just been the one photo, and the caption had only read, “Big Day Today,” so it didn’t clear up much of anything.

Good for him, though. An odd day for it, maybe, but good for him.

A few hours later, though, and suddenly he had a lot more context for the innocent picture.

It started with a picture on Buck’s account, a picture of he and Eddie holding out their left hands together. Wedding bands were obvious on both of them and the caption read, “Presenting: Mr and Mr Diaz.” There were other photos, as well. A photo of them together with Chris, in matching outfits. A photo of Eddie dancing with his mother and then his grandmother, and one of him spinning around with Chris. There was a selfie, him and Eddie pressed together under a white arch, with an exasperated Bobby in the back and Chris squeezed in between them.

They had gotten married.

They had gotten married, and he hadn’t even been told-

(“I have something happening on Christmas, and I would really like for Jee to be there. And you. I asked M-”)

Oh.

Further down the feed was worse.

Eddie’s instagram was filled with Buck and Chris, as always. There was a video of Chris dancing by himself and one of him dancing with Buck. There was a picture of Buck in mid-spin with a screaming Athena while Bobby and Michael laughed off to the side. There was a post with a series of pictures, the first one showing Buck dancing with May, that was captioned: “Stepping on his sisters’ toes.”

The apostrophe placement gave him pause, because that couldn’t be right. Surely Diaz had made a typo, there was no way it could be what it implied. The second photo was of him with May, as was the third, but then the fourth-

Chimney dropped his phone, and then rushed to pick it back up.

It was a picture of Maddie, laughing at Buck’s poor attempt to dance with her. The next was the same, as was the one after it, and the one after that.

But that couldn’t be right, it couldn’t, because surely Buck would have told him if-

(“I have something happening on Christmas, and I would really like for Jee to be there. And you. I asked M-” )

“I asked Maddie,” Chimney said aloud, finishing the cut off word. He hadn’t even let the recording of Buck finish the sentence before deleting the message. He had started deleting his messages after the first couple of lines because surely, surely, if there had been anything important in them, he would have led with that. That message had started with Buck pushing all the blame onto him. “Asked her what? To be there? To come home?”

Had Buck known where she was this entire damn time, and not told him? Or had Buck told him, and he just hadn’t bothered to listen?

He gazed at the pictures, drinking them in like a man dying of thirst in the desert. She was beautiful, she had always been beautiful, but in these photos she seemed… Happy. Carefree. Lighter, maybe. Her hair was cut shorter, and it flared out when she threw her head back to laugh. Chimney hadn’t seen her laughing since… He wasn’t sure. Since before Eddie was shot, perhaps? Since before Jee-Yun was born and Albert had his accident?

But she was there, and he wasn’t.

She was home, and he wasn’t.

She was in Los Angeles, and he was in Boston.

Hen had more photos up, and he panned them for gold. There was a picture of Maddie laughing at Eddie, the man glaring daggers at Taylor while Buck stepped on her toes. A picture of Buck with cake on his face and a smug looking Eddie, while she guffawed beside him. A group picture, with her standing next to May. He found her in the background of one photo, chatting with Eddie’s parents, and then in another, talking to Josh and Taylor together.

Chimney closed out of instagram, and called Maddie, only for it to go directly to voicemail. It made sense, she probably turned it off and forgot to turn it back on. He tried Buck next, because he and Eddie never turned their phones off.

It rang.

And it rang.

And it rang.

And it rang.

And it-

“Hey, it’s Buck, leave a message!”

He ended the call, tried again.

Same results.

He tried again.

It rang once, before he was sent to the mailbox, and he groaned in annoyance. Why couldn’t the little shit just answer his phone? Chimney needed to talk to him. Now. It was important, damn it. The least he could do was answer and-

Oh.

Well. 

That wasn’t a nice feeling to have.

He tried Hen next, and she answered almost immediately.

“Chimney!” she cried, loud in that way she always got when tipsy. There was music playing in the background, competing with laughter and shouts. “The boys got married!”

“I saw,” he said. “Your instagram, I… I saw.”

“They’re so happy, Chim! Why the hell aren’t you here?”

“I didn’t know.”

“Bullshit,” she slurred. “I was standing next to our Buckaroo when he asked you to be here.”

“I didn’t actually listen to the message he left,” he admitted.

“Boy, you have gotten so stupid.”

“I know. Maddie’s there?” he asked, despite knowing the answer.

“Of course! She wouldn’t miss this!”

“Can I… Can I talk to her?”

“Sure!” she said, and then ended the call. 

He stood there for several moments, staring at his phone and debating if he should call her back or wait. But before he could decide, his phone rang with Maddie’s ringtone and Maddie’s name and Maddie’s face and he was slamming his thumb down to accept the call.

“Maddie?” he asked, breathless.

“Howie,” she greeted him. Her voice sounded… Odd. Not as friendly as he’d been hoping. “Why aren’t you here with our daughter?”

“Because I’m in Boston, looking for you,” Chimney answered. “Where have you been? Are you okay?”

“I’m fine, Howie,” she told him. “I left to get help. I got help.”

“I could have helped you, Maddie, if you had stayed and-”

“You weren’t helping me, you were enabling me. I needed someone who would support me, and not just let me keep lying to myself that everything was okay when it wasn’t,” she said. “Because it wasn’t, Howie. It wasn’t. Jee could have been seriously hurt.”

“That wasn’t your fault.”

“I know it wasn’t, but it doesn’t make it any less true.” She sighed. “Why aren’t you here?”

“Because I was an idiot and didn’t listen to Buck when-”

“I don’t mean just today,” she interrupted. “I meant, this entire time. I asked you to stay here with Jee.”

“Because you needed me, and I-”

“I needed you to take care of our daughter. I needed you to give me time to get better, for her and for you and for myself.” He heard Maddie sigh again. “This isn’t a conversation for over the phone at my brother’s wedding.”

“Listen, I’ll get in the car right now and-”

“Stop, Howie. I’m not staying in LA. I’m going back to the clinic in Boston at the end of the week. We’ll talk then.”

“Okay, great!” He was finally going to see her, he was finally going to have her in his arms. Everything was okay, it was a goddamn Christmas miracle. “I’ll be here waiting. Jee and I both will be.”

“Good, I’ve missed her. I’ve missed you both. But Howie? We’re going to talk about why you punched my brother,” she told him, and a shiver ran down his spine. “And there had better be a better answer than just, ‘you were upset.’ Because I will not be there again.”

The call ended and Chimney sat on the hotel bed, clutching his phone tightly. There were tears prickling at his eyes as he stared at Jee, sleeping soundly in her carrier.

“Well,” he said, a dawning realization turning his stomach into writhing snakes. “Daddy’s really messed up this time.”
