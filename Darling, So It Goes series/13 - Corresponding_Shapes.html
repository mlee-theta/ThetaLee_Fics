Corresponding Shapes (Like Puzzle Pieces)

February 2023

The last couple of months had been strained, to say the least. Hectic, chaotic. Ravi switched back to the 118 from the peer  instructor role in January, just in time for Buck to miss an entire week thanks to that Leadership Academy. Which meant, of course, that he was immediately thrust into being Eddie’s partner on the field. And that was, well. Both Diazes tended to be grumpy whenever their partner was absent for any reason. 

Thankfully, he and Albert were not like that. Sure, Ravi missed his boyfriend when he wasn’t there, but he also occasionally worked on opposite shifts from Albert. Honestly, Ravi saw those days as akin to mini-vacations, because A-shift was just a bit too much, sometimes.

For example, not only was there the week that Ravi had to deal with a grumpy Eddie constantly being annoyed with his lack of telepathy - sorry, Eddie, but simply holding out a hand without a word did not convey any message in any language that Ravi was familiar with - but there was also Bobby. Bobby who, apparently, lost a friend on the very first call of the month, back in December. He was also convinced that the man was murdered, so he’d been a bit focused on that.

That was everything at work, though. Outside of it? Outside of it, Chimney and Maddie apparently rushed through the process of buying a years-abandoned house. Easy, considering no one had wanted it, but - and Ravi did want to stress this point - it had been abandoned for several years. Which meant that all the rest of them had been recruited to not only help them move in, but to also make the place habitable again.

It was always impressive what a firehouse full of people could accomplish, and how quickly they could accomplish it, once sufficiently bribed with food.

There was also Buck and Eddie’s deal, because apparently the baby that had been dropped off at the station way back in March ended up in the Wilsons’ custody again around the same time as Bobby’s friend died. There was absolutely nothing about what followed that surprised Ravi. He’d seen the way Buck had looked at the tiny bundle, and he’d seen the way Eddie had looked at Buck looking at the tiny bundle. It was more of a surprise that the baby hadn’t immediately gone home with them that day.

Ravi just really wished that the caseworker hadn’t called him as a reference in the middle of work. It was very intimidating to have Bobby and Eddie and Buck staring him down as he sung the Diazes’ praises. He’d watched Buck pour fresh, steaming, still-technically-boiling coffee directly into his mouth - more than once - and been threatened with an ax by Eddie, so it was already asking a lot of Ravi to convince the woman on the phone that the two men should be trusted with the wellbeing of a tiny human. Being deathglared the entire time just made him flustered on top of it.

By the morning of February the 14th, Buck and Eddie were still trying to figure out regular childcare for their newest addition and Chimney was talking about inviting everyone for a housewarming.

“After we’ve finished unpacking,” Chimney added, a bit sheepishly, as he closed his locker. “We’re still digging out kitchenware.”

“The kitchen stuff was one of the first things we unpacked,” Buck said, leaning against the locker he shared with his husband.

“You mean one of the first things Abuela unpacked,” Eddie corrected.

“Same thing.”

“So,” Hen said, zipping up her bag. “What's everyone’s plans for tonight?”

“Watching my sister’s kids,” Jones said, speaking up so suddenly that Ravi startled a little. The driver-engineer was standing right behind him, and Ravi had entirely forgotten he was there. “I haven’t gotten to spend time with them since Christmas.”

“Aw, that’s sweet,” Hen cooed.

“They’re brats,” Jones said, closing his locker. “But I love ‘em anyway.”

“We’re going out,” Eddie said, shouldering his own bag. Buck did the same beside him, almost mirroring the motion. “Abuela’s pretty much forcing us. There was the threat of bodily harm and everything.”

“Something about her and Pepa wanting the chance to spoil her new bisnieto behind our backs,” Buck added. “Hen, are you and Karen still-?”

“If you think there is anything that will stop my wife from seeing Gavin again in person,” Hen said, cutting him off. “Then you’re even stupider than I thought.”

“We’ll see you tomorrow, then.”

“Damn right. We’ll bring the wine.”

“Well, Maddie and I are staying in,” Chimney said, the words coming out with a bit of a despairing sigh. “Since the Lees have their own plans, and the Diazes are being bullied by an elderly woman. Unless her other, possibly better uncles…?”

Chimney fixed his brother with a hopeful stare, leaning in a little and clasping his hands together. Ravi withheld the sigh he wanted to let out. Because as much as he adored Jee-Yun, he didn’t really want to spend the evening babysitting. But Albert would probably-

“Actually,” Albert said, “we have plans, too.”

“We do?” Ravi blurted out.

“Yeah. They were supposed to be a surprise.”

Albert cut a quick glance at Buck who was, for whatever reason, smirking at them.

“The four of you are the absolute worst uncles,” Chimney declared with a huff. Then he side-eyed Hen. “Maybe her aunts…?”

“Absolutely not.” Hen shook her head. “Denny’s going to a friend’s for some school project, and Karen and I are going out to dinner. And then maybe staying in for dinner, if we have time.”

“The worst aunts,” Chimney complained. Then his eyes landed on Jones as the man was heading for the exit. “Jones! You’re already-”

“No,” he said simply, and was out of the room before Chimney could stammer out a response.

Albert grabbed Ravi’s hand and started dragging him away. Ravi managed to catch a glimpse of Buck and Eddie having one of their nonverbal conversations, and then Buck let out a sigh.

“Okay, how about…” was about all that Ravi heard, before he and Albert were too far away.

“We have plans?” he asked, slotting his fingers in between Albert’s.

“We do.”

“What are they?”

“A surprise.” Albert smirked at him.

“Do you have surprise plans for the whole day?” Ravi asked him, curious. He hit the fob on his keys; his car beeped as it unlocked.

“Well, no,” Albert admitted. He untangled their fingers and went to the other side of the car, throwing his bag into the back. “I figured right now, we just head home and take a nap.”

“And if I’m not tired enough for a nap?” Ravi asked, folding his arms on top of the car’s roof.

“Then I think,” Albert said, smirk widening, “that we should get some cardio in until you are.”

“Get in the car,” Ravi demanded, nearly hitting himself in his rush to throw the door open. His boyfriend laughed at him.

Ravi had no clue what Albert’s plans actually were, even hours later. After an active morning, followed by a lazy afternoon - both spent in bed - his boyfriend suddenly bolted upright, hurriedly dressed, and ran out the door. No explanation was given, just a,

“Wear something nice!”

Ravi, more than a bit bemused, reluctantly pulled himself into the shower before digging through their shared closet for clothes that would be considered “nice.” Not that he knew how nice he was supposed to be going for. Would a button down do? Or should he dig out the suit he wore to the Wilsons’ vow renewal last year?

He’d settled for somewhere halfway, and was in the middle of doing up his tie, when Albert returned.

“So where did you-” Ravi started to ask, stepping out of their bedroom and into the living room. He stopped, both speaking and walking, when he spotted his boyfriend. And what his boyfriend was holding. “Oh.”

“You have no idea,” Albert said, holding out the (very large) bouquet of roses, “how difficult it was to find these this late.”

“Very,” Ravi guessed as he took the flowers, almost cradling them.

“Buck was very insistent that I get you flowers,” Albert told him, leaning in for a peck on the lips that got distinctly more involved very quickly.

“Buck, huh?” Ravi asked when they broke apart. “Buck told you to get me flowers.”

“He was very insistent on it when I asked him for advice,” his boyfriend confessed. “I was gonna get chocolate or something, too, but I figure we can raid the discount bins tomorrow.” Albert paused to look him over, and then he did it again. “You look nice.”

Ravi glanced down at what he was wearing - a white button down with a red tie - and smiled brightly.

“Thank you.”

“Okay, I’ve gotta shower and change,” Albert said, rushing off again. “We have places to be!”

Ravi shook his head at the other man, and then found himself smiling down at the roses in his hand. He hadn’t exactly been someone who cared about getting flowers before, he was always the one giving the flowers in his past relationships, but now that he was on the other side of it, it was… Nice.

No wonder girls liked it so much. No wonder Buck and Eddie got each other flowers all the time.

He dug out his phone and took a picture of the bouquet, shooting it off to Buck without a caption. Buck, in turn, replied with,

👍 👍 👍

good 2 c he listens

tel him he gets 2 live

Do u no where were going?

somewhere fancy 🥳

Ravi frowned at Buck’s text, and then down at his clothes. Just how fancy were they talking? Which then begged the question of what Buck considered “fancy.” It could be anything from a place that offered a five course meal with servings the size of dimes and names straight out of an arthouse catalog, to some place that used cloth napkins and swept the peanut shells off the floor every day instead of once a week.

“Screw it,” Ravi said aloud with a shrug, and busied himself with trying to find something to put the flowers in.

“You put them in a plastic cup?” Albert asked, laughing a little, when he came out of the bedroom a bit later. Ravi was relieved to see that he wasn’t any more dressed up than himself, and was wearing a blue button down with a bright yellow tie. “Wait, shit, we don’t have a vase.”

“We do not,” Ravi confirmed, sliding off his perch on the counter. “Buck says you get to live.”

“Sweet. C’mon, we gotta go, I don’t want to be late.”

“Where are we going?” Ravi tried, grabbing his keys off the table, and then said at the same time as Albert,

“It’s a surprise.”

“Okay, obviously you’re taking me somewhere to eat,” he said, waiting for Albert to lock up behind them before leading the way to the stairs. “You told me to dress fancy, so it’s definitely not, I dunno. Disneyland.”

“I’ve never been to Disneyland,” Albert said, snatching up Ravi’s hand again. “Or any Disney park.”

“Never?”

“Nope.”

“Huh.” Something to keep in mind. “Okay, well, it’s Valentine’s Day, you asked Buck for advice, you told me to dress nice-”

Ravi came to a sudden halt, causing Albert to stumble a little beside him. He glanced down at his clothes, then at Albert’s, with narrowed eyes.

“You asked Buck for advice…” he said again.

“Yeah?” Albert stared at him. “What about that is-?” His eyes went wide. “No! No, no, no. Noooo. It’s not that! It is absolutely not that! I love you, Rav, but it’s only been a year and-”

Ravi shut him up with one of the more effective ways he had found: kissing him. Albert was left blinking in confusion when they parted again.

“You love me?”

“What? O- Oh. Uh… Yes?”

“I love you, too,” he said, and watched the smile blossom on Albert’s face. “And to be perfectly clear, you’re not surprising me with a wedding in Bobby’s backyard?”

“No!” Albert frantically shook his head. They were still close enough that it meant their noses knocked together. “No, no, absolutely not.”

“Thank God.”

“I- I mean, I love you, I just-”

“Yeah, no, me neither.”

“Okay.”

“Okay.”

There was an awkward silence for a moment, and then another, and then another. Ravi wetted his lips, rocked on his heels, and broke it with,

“So, Alley Cat, where are we going?”

“A restaurant,” Albert answered, leaping on the subject change. He led the way as they started for the car again. “Buck knows the owner, somehow, so he got us reservations.”

“Buck knows the owner,” Ravi repeated, trying to understand that piece of info. “How does Buck know the owner of some fancy, need-a-reservation, restaurant?”

“I didn’t ask.” They reached the exit door; Albert held it open for Ravi. “Chimney’s told enough stories about how Buck used to be that I know enough to know that I didn’t want to know more.”

“Yeah, okay, that’s fair.”

“He said the bread was to die for, though…”

Neither one of them, thankfully, managed to choke on any dinner rolls.

They did, however, have to deal with Bobby’s look of infinite disapproval when they both walked into the station for their next shift with hickeys that were a bit too high up to be covered by their shirts. They weren’t alone, however: Hen was right next to them, facing their captain’s silent disappointment for the same thing.

 

Fin.
