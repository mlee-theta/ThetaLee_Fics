Against the Tide We Struggle

Here’s the thing: It would never have happened if Eddie hadn’t left.

It was April, and Eddie had left them four months ago. It was April, and Buck hadn’t spoken to Eddie since their last shouting match in January. It was April, and Buck was once again sitting on a hospital bed waiting to be discharged.

The 118 had responded to a structure fire earlier that day. An apartment building, long past when it should have been deemed inhospitable. Bobby had split the team up, sent them in teams of two to different floors to clear them as quickly as possible. He’d sent Buck up to the top floor, along with one of the many floaters that had come in to fill the empty spots that had swiss-cheesed their team in recent months.

Buck didn’t even know the floater’s name. It was something Jenkins, he knew that. Just like he also knew there’d be another floater next month.

They’d gone through, pounding on each door they passed and sending anyone who answered scurrying for the stairs. The fire was fast approaching, and smoke was filling the hallway. But everything had been… Not fine. Nothing had been fine since Christmas, but things hadn’t been dire. Not truly. Not until Buck took a step and felt more than heard the floor creak and give a little. He froze, held up a hand, told him, “Something’s wrong.”

If it had been Eddie there with him, it would never have happened.

If it had been Eddie there with him, the other man would have frozen and not moved an inch. Buck wouldn’t have even needed to have said something was wrong, he would have just known from the way Buck stopped mid-step. They would have looked at each other, neither needing to say a thing to let the other know what they were thinking. And then they would have carefully set about getting themselves out of the situation they’d found themselves in.

It wasn’t Eddie, though. It was Jenkins. Jenkins, who shifted his weight and took a step forward and-

And suddenly the floor was collapsing under Buck’s feet and he was falling, O2 tank smashing into his back as his back smashed through one floor and then another and then another. He landed, winded and dazed, right in front of the main exit. Unwilling to move, unable to move, as Hen and Bobby rushed to his side and loaded him up.

His luck had held better than the floors, at least. A minor concussion, a dislocated shoulder, a mapwork of bruises. It was definitely worse than when he’d ended up in the same bed because of another call the month before, or when he’d needed stitches before that, or - Okay, maybe Buck had been playing fast and loose with the concept of personal safety lately, but he wasn’t really needed as a back up plan anymore, was he? But it was once again nothing life threatening. Nothing broken. He’d been set to be discharged, as soon as one of his emergency contacts got there to take him home.

Which was a hilarious concept, because his emergency contacts were either a) all the way in New England, still; b) busy overseeing the clean up of the fire he’d been injured in; or c) pissed off at him for voicing his valid concerns and also too busy to come get him. So he’d settled in, fully expecting to be waiting hours until Bobby or Athena had the time to play taxi for him.

Except, apparently he was wrong. Apparently, his assessment of Option C hadn’t been entirely correct. Because he looked up as the curtain moved and Eddie came rushing in, looking ridiculous in his Starbucks uniform. He’d looked tired and thinner than he had months before. Smaller.

“What are you doing here?” was the first thing out of Buck’s mouth. It hadn’t even been said rudely, is the thing. He’d expected to be far angrier upon seeing his (former?) friend for the first time in months, but he was honestly just surprised. It was an honest question asked with honest surprise. The way Eddie’s head jerked back, though, one would think Buck had thrown a drink in his face.

“They said you were hurt.”

“Okay,” Buck nodded, wincing as it made his headache worse. “But why are you here?”

“Buck, you’re hurt,” Eddie said, voice sounding strangled.

“It’s not the first time I’ve been hurt. You didn’t rush in here for those times.”

“I was working.”

Buck raised an eyebrow and sent a pointed look at the Starbucks uniform. Eddie glanced down, blushed, and shuffled his feet.

“Bobby sounded worried when he-”

“Oh, so you’ll find the time to answer his calls?”  Buck snapped, and there was the anger that was missing.

“He sounded worried and said you’d probably need a ride home,” Eddie continued, crossing his arms to shield himself. “Why isn’t Taylor here to do it?”

“We broke up.”

“What?” Eddie looked stunned. “When? Why? I thought the two of you-”

“If you’d been my friend these past couple of months, you’d know the when and the why,” Buck snarled. He really did not feel like digging into that fresh wound, not with the way his head pounded, not with the way seeing Eddie again physically pained him. “When are they discharging me?”

“Now,” the other man said. “We just- The discharge papers, we just need to grab them.”

“Let’s get going then, so you can go back to ignoring me in favor of making overly expensive coffees.”

He stood and brushed past Eddie, ignoring the look of hurt that played across his face.

It was January and Eddie was cleaning out his locker and Buck was trying to get him to understand. He was throwing himself again and again at the rockwall of the man’s stubbornness, trying desperately to get him to listen. He’d been doing it the entire time, all the days and the weeks, since Christmas.

“Eddie, would you stop and listen to-?”

“No, Buck, I won’t. I’ve made my decision, you just have to get over it.”

“No, I won’t, because you aren’t listening!” He ran his hand through his hair in frustration, the curls long since torn from their styling. “You literally cannot afford to do this.”

“It will be fine, I know what I’m doing.”

“No you literally don’t!” Buck shouted, grabbing him by the shoulder and forcing him to face him. “You have no idea what this will do to Chris, Eds, you haven’t even thought-”

“I’m doing this for Chris!” Eddie shouted back.

“No, you’re panicking and running away!” Buck shouted back. It was just them in the locker room, but the rest of the station had gone utterly silent. “And you’re not thinking of the consequences!”

“What? What consequences? Consequences like Chris being able to sleep at night?”

“Consequences like losing Chris!” Buck snapped. “Consequences like hurting him because you’re making a stupid-”

“Don’t tell me how to raise Christopher!” Eddie roared, making Buck stumble over his feet as he shoved him out of his space. “He’s my son to worry about, not yours!”

And Buck had just… Shut down, the words etching themselves into his memory as he turned and left Eddie to it. He ignored the looks from everyone in the station bay as he stomped to the bunks, the words playing on repeat.

“My son…”

(“Because, Evan…”)

“...not yours.”

(“Because, Evan…”)

“My son.”

(“Because, Evan…”)

“Not yours.”

(“Because, Evan…”)

The drive to Buck’s apartment was silent and awkward. The only time he spoke to Eddie was to correct him on where to go after the man started heading for the loft. He ignored the man’s confused questions, only telling him where to turn and where to park when they arrived at the building his new apartment was in.

“When did you move from the loft?”

Silence, as Buck pressed the elevator button for his floor.

“This is closer to Abuela’s.”

Silence, as Buck unlocked his door.

“It looks nice.”

Silence, as Buck filled himself a glass of water to take his ibuprofen.

“Okay, what’s your problem, man?” Eddie finally snapped.

“You!” Buck slammed the glass on the counter, splattering water everywhere. Copeland had told him, a year ago, to be more honest with his feelings. So that’s what he’ll do. “You’re my problem! You promised you’d have my back, but you didn’t! You promised that what happened to Red wouldn’t happen to us, but it did! You left!”

“I didn’t-” Eddie looked to be struggling for words. “I didn’t meant to leave you, I just- I have to work more, because-”

“Because you quit your job before thinking it through,” Buck finished for him. “Because affording rent is a lot more difficult when you’ve burnt through your savings and you’re suddenly making half as much as you were, if that. Like I warned you. And tell me, Eddie, did it help with anything?”

Eddie looked away and didn’t answer.

“This happened today-” He tugged on the sling his arm was in. “-because you weren’t there to have my back. If it had been you there with me, instead of that fucking idiot Jenkins-”

“Who’s Jenkins?”

“Then I wouldn’t have ended up falling through four floors. I got hurt today, because you weren’t there to have my back, because you left.”

“I just wanted to do what was best for Chris.”

“Hate to break it to you, Eddie, but you didn’t. He’s still as anxious as he was before Christmas.” Buck caught the look of surprise on the other man’s face. “Did you not know that? Or did you think that just because you weren’t talking to me, that Christopher wouldn’t, either?”

“I… I wanted to talk to you, after- But I was just so busy, trying to make things work with the bills and the groceries and- And I just didn’t have the time.”

“Was it worth it?”

“No,” Eddie admitted, digging into his pocket and pulling out a folded sheet of paper. He threw it onto the kitchen island. “You were right. About everything.”

One-handed, Buck reached down and unfolded it. Immediately, he focused on the large red letters glaring up at him: “EVICTION.”

“Oh, god damn it, Eddie.”

“You were right, you were fucking right,” he said, burying his face in his hands. “I kept coming up short on rent and I couldn’t ask Pepa or Abuela and all the jobs that would hire me don’t pay for shit. And my parents, you know the moment they hear about this that they’ll come swooping in and take Chris. I won’t be able to stop them, either, because the state won’t let me keep him if I can’t keep a roof over his head and-”

Buck laid his hand on his shoulder, silencing him.

“Follow me,” he told him.

Back in January, after that awful argument in the station locker room, Buck had realized that Eddie was never going to listen. So, instead of preventative measures, he opted to start looking for a larger apartment. It was part of what led to the break up with Taylor, the fact that he was looking for a new place to live with Christopher in mind and not her. 

“It’s a hair smaller than his room now, I think,” Buck said, opening the door to reveal the bedroom he’d decorated with glow in the dark stars and posters of galaxies. “But he said he liked it, when Abuela brought him over.”

“You- You moved, so that Chris had a place to stay?”

“I moved so that Chris and you would have a place to stay,” Buck corrected. “I would have gotten a three bedroom, but as you’ve been brutally reminded, the housing market in this city is not kind. ‘Sides, I figured I didn’t have time to be picky. You can sleep on the couch, it’s still the pull out.”

“Buck, why? Why do all of this?”

“Because I promised to have your back,” he told him. “And I know he isn’t my son, but I still worry about him.”

“I shouldn’t have said that,” Eddie said, face falling at the use of his own words. “I should not have- I am so sorry.”

“Why? You weren’t wrong.”

“Evan, you left the loft you loved to move into this apartment, just to make sure Chris had somewhere to sleep,” Eddie told him, grabbing him by the uninjured shoulder, thumb warm against his pulse. “Nothing I have ever said has been more wrong.”

“Agree to disagree,” Buck told him, stepping out of his hold and heading back to the kitchen. “We’ll see about getting your things moved here or put into storage later. Do you have to get back to work? Because I’m thinking about ordering something, I am starving.”

He heard Eddie sigh, before he started following.
