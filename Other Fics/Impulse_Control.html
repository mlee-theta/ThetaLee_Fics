Impulse Control

"I'm leaving the one-eighteen." Eddie said it so definitively, as if there was no conceivable alternative. The other three - Hen and Buck and Ravi - all stared at him dumbly for several moments, before Buck finally broke the silence.

"Wait, you're leaving because of what Chris said?"

"Yes. I just... I want to be safe for him, so that he isn't so worried."

"That's nice and all," Hen commended. "But are you dumb?"

"Excuse me?"

"Do you even have another job lined up? When did you even decide this?" she asked, and Buck found himself nodding along.

"Last night, or maybe this morning," Eddie said. "Bobby said he was going to try and get me a job with admin, or the academy, or maybe as a fire marshal."

"Eds, do you have any idea how much of a pay cut that will be?" Buck asked. "You're a single father to a special needs kid in one of the most expensive cities in the world to live in. When I was stuck as a fire marshal, I got by with the money I had saved up while traveling around. You don't have that. What are you going to do about your rent? About the tuition for Chris' school?"

"I... I hadn't thought that far ahead," Eddie admitted.

"And you all call me impulsive..."

"But you said you talked to Bobby," Hen said.

"I did."

"And he didn't ask you any of this?"

Eddie shook his head.

"Was he drunk?" Ravi asked, incredulous, and then immediately wilted as the other three glared at him.

"We don't make those jokes," Buck told him darkly. "But he's right, what the hell was he- Hen, where are you going?"

"To yell at the other idiot while you continue yelling at this one," she told them as she marched away.

"I don't see what the big deal is," Eddie said, throwing up his hands. "Chris is freaked out about me possibly dying, and I just want to be safe for him."

"He watched you collapse to the floor while shopping for suits," Buck told him. "I don't think his fear of you dying is as connected to the job as you think it is, especially since we don't tell him the details of the actually dangerous calls we go on."

"What do you want me to do instead?" Eddie demanded, crossing his arms tightly across his chest.

"Therapy?" Ravi suggested, and Buck excitedly waved his hands at him.

"Exactly! You took him to a therapist when he started having nightmares after the tsunami, why is it different this time?"

"I didn't think about that, either," Eddie said, looking down. "Chris just said what he said about- Y'know. And I just..."

"You panicked," Buck finished for him.

"I didn't panic."

"It sounds like you panicked," Ravi said, and then flinched back from the glare the older man sent him.

"I didn't panic," Eddie repeated, gritting his teeth.

"Eddie..." Buck's voice was gentle, and his friend slumped with a sigh.

"Okay, fine, I panicked," he said.

"You haven't actually filed paperwork for anything yet, right?" Buck asked, sighing in relief when the other man shook his head. "Good, that's good. It's not the end of the world, then."

"What are you going to do instead?" Ravi asked, and Eddie shrugged.

"Find a therapist for Chris, I guess."

"And yourself," Buck told him. "No, shut up. I should have pushed you about it months ago, but you were still healing and then the black out happened and I just let it slip through my fingers."

"It's not your fault, Buck."

"It is, because I promised to have your back, and clearly I did not," he said, throwing an arm around Eddie's shoulder. "But now I will. We'll find a therapist for Chris, and a therapist for you."

"Therapy for everyone," Ravi chimed in.

"Therapy for everyone," Buck agreed.

"I'll hate every moment of it," Eddie promised.

"I'd expect nothing less."
