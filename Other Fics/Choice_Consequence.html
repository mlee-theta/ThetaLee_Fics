Chapter 1: Buck

Once upon a time, when Buck saw his future and recoiled in fear, Eddie told him, “that won’t happen to us.” It wasn’t a pinky promise, but Buck had heard it as the oath it was all the same. It was a shame, though, that neither of them had thought about the axiom of a first responder: Don’t make promises.

 

On a bright and sunny Christmas day, Eddie gathered them all together and crushed their holiday cheer with this simple sentence:

 

“I’m leaving the 118.”

 

And Buck, he understood. Or, he tried to understand. Christopher was frightened of losing another parent. Eddie was leaving to be safe for his son, so his son could sleep soundly knowing his father would come home.

 

Eddie was still leaving, though.

 

At least he tried to keep his promise, the one he made while Buck was spiraling over Red. He tried very hard, even as he left the station to take an admin job elsewhere in the department, and Buck had to take more hours to make up for the loss of yet another firefighter. They carved out time for Buck to spend with Eddie and Chris, if they could, or just him and one of the Diazes if they couldn’t. They tried, is the point.

 

But Los Angeles is an expensive city to live in, and Christopher had expensive needs. When the combination of rent and tuition started devouring his savings, Eddie got a second job to make ends meet. Insurance covered Carla, which was a blessing, because she was now helping with Christopher nearly as much as she had been before.

 

Soon, trying to make schedules align to actually be together became an Olympic level sport, and other portions of Buck’s life began to suffer for it.

 

“You’re ditching me to hang out with your friend and his kid?” Taylor’s tone was incredulous and offended. “You just saw Chris yesterday.”

 

“But this is the first time all week that Eddie’s time off matches mine.”

 

“I came over to see you, Buck. My boyfriend. I didn’t come over to be at my boyfriend’s apartment by myself.”

 

“I didn’t ask you to come over. What do you want me to do, just ditch them and stay here?”

 

“Yes!” the reporter snapped, and the short moment of silence that followed was heavy. “I hardly ever see you anymore, and whatever free time you have seems to always be spent with one of them.”

 

“What do you want me to do here, Taylor?” Buck asked again.

 

“Choose.”

 

“Choose?”

 

“Yes! Me, or them.”

 

Much to Taylor’s shock and heartbreak, the choice wasn’t even a choice at all. And perhaps the most shocking and heartbreaking part was that Buck wasn’t shocked, nor was he heartbroken. Not by the choice, nor by the consequence. Taylor gathered her things and walked out the door, and he didn’t see her again unless he turned on the news.

 

And Buck felt fine with that.

 

He wasn’t fine when Eddie realized that, even with the department admin job and the part-time job at a grocery store, he was still living on a prayer and the handouts of family between most paychecks. He wasn’t fine when Eddie picked up a third job manning a desk at a hotel most nights. He wasn’t fine when, suddenly, there was absolutely no time in their schedules to see each other.

 

Buck wasn’t fine when he realized that the promise Eddie had made was a promise Eddie couldn’t keep.

 

“Have you talked to him at all?” Hen asked him on one of the suddenly numerous nights she had Christopher for a sleepover. Buck was over too, taking the chance to see his Superman while he could.

 

“When? I barely get a text back these days. I haven’t actually laid eyes on the man in over a week, and that was just long enough for him to hand off Chris.”

 

“He’s doing it for Chris,” Hen reminded him, and Buck knew that. He did. He kept reminding himself that when a text went unanswered for hours at a time, when calls rang and rang and went to voicemail, when he barely had time to ask Eddie how he was doing in the five seconds he spent in Buck’s doorway.

 

“I know he’s doing it for Chris,” Buck said. “But now he’s gone, too.”

 

It was for Christopher, it was so Christopher could sleep soundly and not be so afraid of losing another parent, and so Buck buckled down and bit his tongue and tried to be fine.

 

But Christopher wasn’t fine.

 

According to the teachers at school, whenever Buck was the one to do pick-up, the boy had grown silent and sullen. According to Carla, when she was the one dropping him off on her way home or to another client, he laughed less and no longer shared stories of his day. According to Hen, while they were relaxing between calls, Denny had caught him crying in the middle of the night.

 

On one of the days it was Buck’s turn to have him, he confronted him about it. Gently, of course. Carefully, while they were piecing together one of the many Lego sets that had migrated to the loft.

 

“Has something been bugging you?” he asked. “Because Carla, and Auntie Hen, and your teachers, they all think something’s bothering you.”

 

“I barely see Dad anymore,” Chris told him.

 

“I know, kiddo,” Buck said, wrapping an arm around him. “He’s doing it for you, though.”

 

“But he could die, and I won’t have seen him at all!” Chris shouted, knocking the Legos to the floor with a sweep of his hand.

 

“No- Chris-” He was stumbling for his words. “He’s not a firefighter anymore, he’s safer now.”

 

“Mommy wasn’t a firefighter either,” Chris answered, and it was the last thing he said that night. 

 

Somewhere along the way, Buck was starting to realize, everyone had forgotten to actually ask Christopher… Anything. Anything at all. Eddie had seen his child in pain and afraid and instead of talking it out, he made a choice and stuck to it. And Buck had let him, because he’d been doing it for his son. 

 

It was unsettling, to see a situation so utterly familiar, but from the other side of it all.

 

The next day, after dropping Chris off at school and texting Bobby he’ll be late, he stormed to the department HQ. Buck almost felt bad for confronting Eddie at work, but then again, when was he ever not at work? Eyes watched him as he stomped along halls and past office doors, but no one who saw the storm in his eyes dared to stop him.

 

Eddie’s desk and everything on it rattled when Buck’s fist slammed down on it.

 

“What the hell?”

 

“You left,” Buck said, words acid on his tongue. “You said it wasn’t going to happen to us, and then you left.”

 

“Buck,” the man sighed. “We talked about this.”

 

“I know, and it was fine when it was just me. Suck it up and move on, right?” And a vicious part of him took petty glee in seeing Eddie’s eyes widen at that. “But you didn’t just leave me, Eddie. You left Christopher.”

 

“I’m doing this for Christopher,” Eddie snarled, temper rising.

 

“Are you?” Buck asked. “Did you even ask him what he wanted? Have you actually talked to your son since Christmas? Have you actually seen him this week?”

 

Eddie stayed silent, and that was enough of an answer.

 

“He’s crying at night,” Buck told him. “He doesn’t talk to anyone, he doesn’t laugh, and he’s just as afraid of losing you as he was before Christmas. He’s already lost you.”

 

“But I’m safer now.”

 

“Christopher knows damn well you don’t need to be a firefighter to die, Eds. His mother taught him that.”

 

“What do you want me to do, Evan?”

 

“I don’t know, talk to your son? Be a father? Stop running away? Actually see a therapist?” To every option, Eddie cringed like he was being punched. “I can’t tell you what to do, I’m barely even your friend at this point. This is the most we’ve spoken, face to face, in weeks.”

 

“I… I’m sorry.”

 

“I’m sorry too,” he told him. “And now I have to go, because I’m ten minutes late for my shift.”

 

“Oh," he said, and then, with a hopeful note to the words, "Talk to you later, then?”

 

“When?” Buck scoffed, and didn’t linger for Eddie’s fumbling answer.

Chapter 2: Eddie

Chapter Notes

Everything he had done, from the moment Shannon left him that note and walked out the door, had been for Chris. But it was for an entirely different reason now. He watched as the door cracked open, as the blond peaked in. He watched as his son crept out and back into the living room.

 

As his son hugged him and apologized for shouting, as Buck joined them on the couch, he thought to himself: Everything’s going to be fine.

 

And, for the first time in months, the words didn’t feel like a lie.

Chapter 3: Chris

Chapter Notes

Look, when I first made this story, it was entirely intended to be a one shot. But now I've written two chapters in the span of a few hours, so obviously that didn't last. I'm going to keep this story marked as complete, just like it has been, but at this point there's no telling when I'll get hit by inspiration wielding a mallet.

When Christopher was really, really little, he woke up one day to his mom being gone. For so long afterwards, it had just been him and his dad and his grandparents. Actually, mostly, it was him and his grandparents. His dad worked all the time and he was hardly ever home. When he was home, he was so tired. He tried to hide it, but Chris could tell.

 

His dad had tried to hide how he and Chris’ grandparents would fight, too, but Chris heard them.

 

One day, after one of those fights, one where he heard his grandparents yelling, his dad asked if he wanted to go on a trip together. He told Chris that he missed him all the time. He promised that he would never leave him again.

 

Moving to Los Angeles was maybe the best thing ever. His bisabuela was there, and so was Tia Pepa. His dad got a new job, one where he helped people, like a superhero, and he got to see him far more often than he ever did in Texas. In Los Angeles, they met his Bucky and Ms. Carla. In Los Angeles, he got to do so many new things and started at an awesome school and no one cared about his crutches or his CP.

 

And then, on his first Christmas in LA, his mom came back.

 

The months that followed were so great. He had his dad, he had his mom, he had his Bucky; he had his whole family. Everything was great and he was so happy.

 

And then, his mom died.

 

And then, Bucky got hurt.

 

Chris wanted to cry, he wanted to scream, to shout. But that never worked. His dad was so strong and big and he was a hero, and he wasn’t crying or screaming or shouting. So Chris decided he wouldn’t either. He wanted to be strong like his dad, after all.

 

One day, after his Bucky got better and then got sick again, he and Chris went to the pier. It was such a fun day, they played games and went on rides and took photos. And Bucky talked to him, not like he was some stupid kid but like he was an adult, like him.

 

The tsunami that followed was scary, but he had his Bucky. His Bucky kept him safe.

 

Chris didn’t understand everything that followed. His dad took him to see Bucky, and then Bucky started working again, and his dad was happier. Chris started having nightmares of his mom in the water, and he didn’t tell his dad because he didn’t want to make him sad. And then Bucky stopped coming over and his dad was upset and he didn’t know why.

 

It took forever, it felt like, for him to see Bucky again.

 

After that, though, he had his family again. He got to spend Christmas at the station, and Bucky made him a skateboard that he wouldn’t fall off of. Chris and Bucky and his dad played games or watched movies almost all the time. He missed them, while he was at summer camp, but that was fun too.

 

Everything that followed was not fun.

 

First, he was stuck at home while his dad stayed at Bucky’s and he couldn’t see any of them! And then, he was still stuck at home and had to do his classes online and couldn’t hang out with any of his friends even while they hung out together. He couldn’t see his tia or his bisabuela or Carla. Everyone was gone.

 

He still had his dad, though. And Bucky.

 

And when his dad told him he started seeing someone, as more than a friend, Chris got so upset. What was the point in meeting someone new if they were going to leave, too? He yelled at his dad, he broke a bowl, and he ran away to Bucky. Bucky made him feel better, though. Bucky promised he’d see everyone again, he promised that Chris still had him.

 

And dad’s new girlfriend turned out to be Chris’ old teacher, Ms. Flores, who he had liked!

 

So things were okay, he still got to see Bucky and now he got to see Ms. Flores, and then Carla came back!

 

And then a bad man hurt his dad. He didn’t get hurt in a fire or because of his job. Someone hurt him. On purpose. Bucky cried after he told him, and Chris wanted to cry, too. But his dad didn’t cry, his dad was big and strong and a hero, and Chris wanted to be just like his dad. So he didn’t cry, no matter how much he wanted to.

 

When his dad came home from the hospital, Bucky stayed and helped. Or Ms. Flores did, if Bucky couldn’t. His dad hurt all the time, but he didn’t complain. Sometimes he’d catch Bucky staring at his dad, or his dad staring at Bucky, but only when the other wasn’t paying attention. He didn’t know what that was about, and neither of them said anything, so he didn’t either.

 

When his dad went to work, he wasn’t worried. Bucky would have his back, just like he promised he would. Bucky would keep his dad safe, and his dad would keep Bucky safe. Ms. Flores - Ana - was around more, and it was nice to have another friend. Bucky and Chris would go on zoo trips, just the two of them, and it felt special, like how he’d do things with just his dad.

 

And then his dad fell down while they were shopping for a new suit. He thought he was having a heart attack, and those killed people! His dad could have died, on the floor, in the middle of the store! The doctor told them that it had just been a panic attack, but that didn’t make sense to Chris. His dad was big and strong and a hero; his dad didn’t get scared.

 

After the blackout, when he spent five whole days with Ana, she and dad talked in the kitchen. When she came out, Ana told him goodbye and went out the door and she didn’t come back again.  Chris was upset about it, because it was another person that was gone. But his dad was happier, he thought, so maybe it was a good thing.

 

Once, after Ana left and Bucky hurt his eye, Chris woke up to his dad in bed with him. He was being weird, and he told him so, but he didn’t mind it. He loved his dad, and he said he was okay, so he just enjoyed having him there while he could.

 

And then it was Christmas, their fourth Christmas in Los Angeles, and it had to be perfect this year. Everything had to be perfect. His dad could die at any time. He could have an accident like his mom, or he could be hurt by a bad guy again, or he could have a heart attack in the middle of the store.

 

This Christmas had to be perfect, because his dad could be dead next year.

 

Then his dad stopped being a firefighter, and Chris didn’t understand why. Didn’t his dad like saving people? Didn’t he like being a hero? But his dad stopped being a firefighter, and he acted happy when he told Chris, so Chris tried to be happy for him. Besides, his dad was there now when he came home from school and he was there every weekend, so there was that at least, even if it didn’t last.

 

A couple months after he stopped being a firefighter, he started another job somewhere else, and Chris saw him just as often as before. Sometimes he saw him less. Days that used to be spent with his dad and Bucky became days spent with just one or the other. Carla was watching him just like she had been when his dad had been a firefighter, so he really didn’t understand why he stopped being one if nothing was going to change.

 

And then his dad picked up a third job, and this was not okay. Now Chris spent almost every night either at Bucky’s place or having sleepovers with Denny or with his Abuela. Now Chris hardly ever saw his dad at all. Chris wasn’t happy, and even though he tried to hide it, he knew other people saw it.

 

His dad wasn’t around anymore, even if he hadn’t died, and the thought would make him cry. He tried not to, he tried to be strong like his dad, but he couldn’t help it. He hadn’t realized that Denny had seen him do it.

 

One night afterwards, one of the nights he spent with Bucky building Legos, Bucky tried to talk to him.

 

“Has something been bugging you?” he asked. “Because Carla, and Auntie Hen, and your teachers, they all think something’s bothering you.”

 

“I barely see Dad anymore,” Chris told him. Chris hadn’t seen his dad since the Saturday before, when he left to go to work. It was Wednesday now.

 

“I know, kiddo,” Bucky said, wrapping an arm around him. “He’s doing it for you, though.”

 

“But he could die, and I won’t have seen him at all!” he shouted, knocking the Legos to the floor with a sweep of his hand.

 

“No- Chris- He’s not a firefighter anymore, he’s safer now.”

 

“Mommy wasn’t a firefighter either,” Chris answered, and why didn’t Bucky understand? What did not being a firefighter anymore have to do with his dad being safer? His dad could have an accident, or get hurt by a bad guy again, or have a heart attack. His dad could still die, only now he wasn’t seeing him at all.

 

Chris didn’t talk to Bucky the rest of the night, or the next morning on the way to school. He felt bad about it, but he didn’t see the point if Bucky wasn’t going to understand. He spent his day at school the same as he did the other days at school, staying quiet and keeping to himself, because he was afraid that if he started talking then he’d start screaming, and screaming didn’t help. His dad didn’t scream, his dad didn’t get upset. He wouldn’t either.

 

That day, after school, his dad picked him up. He wasn’t going to work that day, he said. He was going to spend it with Chris, he said. But he should have known something was up, because just as they started watching Finding Dory, his dad tried to talk with him.

 

“Chris,” his dad started to say. “Chris, Buck said… He told me you’re crying at night?”

 

Chris stayed silent and kept his eyes on the movie. He didn’t want to talk to his dad, he was upset with his dad, and he didn’t want to be upset with him.

 

“Chris, c’mon. You can talk to me.”

 

“You’re never here,” he finally said, not able to help himself.

 

“I know, I know, and I’m so sorry for that. But I’m doing it for you, okay? I’m safer now.”

 

“But I never see you!” Chris shouted, getting off the couch. “I never see you and you could die!”

 

“But I’m safer now,” his dad said, but Chris ignored him and went to his room. His Bucky didn’t understand, and his dad didn’t understand, and Chris couldn’t understand why they couldn’t understand. How was his dad any safer now? His mom hadn’t been a firefighter, and she’d had an accident and died. His dad got hurt by a bad guy and nearly died, and he could have a heart attack and die at work or at home or at the store.

 

And Chris was upset, and he wanted to cry, but he wanted to be like his dad. He wanted to be strong like his dad, a hero like his dad used to be. His dad didn’t cry, so Chris didn’t want to cry. He didn’t want to be angry with his dad, either.

 

His door opened, and Bucky’s head popped in.

 

“Hey Superman, your dad said you got upset?”

 

“I yelled at him again.”

 

“Did you break any more salad bowls?” Chris shook his head. “Come on out here, kiddo. Your dad’s gonna order us pizza and we’re gonna watch Disney movies.”

 

So Chris went back to the living room and he apologized to his dad. They finished Finding Dory and then started Luca, and at some point the pizza came and his dad and Bucky left to get plates from the kitchen. He could hear them talking in there, and they were gone long enough that he just ate from the box. His Bucky laughed at that when they came back.

 

After Luca was over, they didn’t start another one right away. Instead, his dad turned to look at him, and Chris thought that he was about to start crying. But his dad didn’t cry.

 

“Chris, I am so sorry,” he said. “I didn’t realize- I was trying to do the right thing, but I made a mistake, and you got hurt. I quit being a firefighter so I could be safer for you, so you wouldn’t have to worry about me dying anymore.”

 

“But you could still die,” Chris said. “Mommy wasn’t a firefighter, and she had an accident and she died! And- And a bad guy could still hurt you, or you could have a heart attack! But now I don’t get to see you anymore.”

 

“Chris...” he heard Bucky say at the same time his dad said, “Oh mijo.”

 

“I’m sorry, I- I didn’t realize-” His dad was crying now, he was. His dad never cried, but he was crying now. “I’m so sorry.”

 

“Your dad and I were talking,” Bucky said, and when Chris looked he seemed like he might be crying too. “And we decided that he wasn’t going to work as much.”

 

“I’m going to quit the other jobs,” his dad said. “And Buck’s going to be around more, to help out.”

 

“Is he going to live with us?”

 

“Uh, maybe?” his dad looked over his head at Bucky. “We’ll see. But we’re both going to be home more, okay?”

 

“And then your dad and you-”

 

“And you,” his dad interrupted.

 

“And me. We’re going to sit down with a doctor, like how you did after the tsunami.”

 

“But I’m going to see you more?” Chris asked, because that was more important.

 

“Yeah, mijo. You will.”

 

“Good,” Chris said, and maybe he was crying too. But so was his dad, so maybe it was okay.
