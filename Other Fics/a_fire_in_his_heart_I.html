a fire in his heart (I gave you mine)

It started like this. A stranger, a woman dressed up as Santa’s helper, saying,

“You two have an adorable son.”

And Buck, instead of correcting her, thanked her.

The feelings had been there from the moment an ambulance exploded behind them, from the moment Buck’s insecurities let go of his thoughts and Eddie went from Possible Replacement to Coworker-Friend-Family. They had been there and they’d taken root deep in his heart as he saw father rush to hug son after the earthquake.

Sometimes, he regrets not doing something, anything, then. Sometimes, he regrets not taking the first step right then and giving words and actions to his feelings and thoughts. But there was still the ghost of Abby, and there was still Shannon. There were many things Buck would happily get between, but a man and his wife? Christopher and his mother? No, never. So instead, Buck tried with Ali while Eddie tried again with Shannon, and then…

And then.

A car accident.

A truck bombing.

Trauma had a splash zone, and Ali wasn’t willing to be in it. She fled, and Buck was left alone, life put on pause, while everyone else around him continued on. He healed, he struggled, he became better than he ever was.

And then he was coughing up blood.

And then Eddie was dragging him out of bed, Christopher serving as the most effective emotional battering ram in history.

And then Christopher was asking where all the water had gone, and Buck was running with his heart in his throat and his heart in his arms and he wasn’t fast enough. Trauma had a splash zone, and all of Los Angeles was in it.

There was Eddie, still trusting him with Chris, even though he’d turned his back and lost him. Chris, who could have died and was only there because of him, because Eddie had trusted him once and he’d failed, and -

“I love him enough to never stop trying, and I know you do too.”

And

“There’s nobody in this world I trust with my son more than you.”

Buck’s heart was fit to burst with the emotions pumping through it.

And then he was ripping Bosko’s name off his locker, he was running from dinner with Athena and Bobby, he was suing Bobby and the department. He was hurt, and he felt alone and replaced, and he was flailing about with no care or thought beyond the absolute refusal to be left behind this time.

There was the Grocery Store Fight, an incident seared into Buck’s mind. There was Eddie, tearing into him, all rage and pain. There was Buck, giving in the moment Chris was brought up, because God, God, no he hadn’t thought about how it would affect Chris, honestly never once thought that he would miss him.

Shame bubbled in his gut, shame and regret and love for a kid that wasn’t his, and that more than anything led to him dropping the suit.

It continued like this, after lawsuits and fight clubs, after flaring insecurities and simmering anger, after apologies and forgiveness. It continued with Chris, after being told he couldn’t spend Christmas with his father, turning around and asking to spend it with Buck. And Buck...

(“You two have an adorable son.”)

(“You know how much Christopher misses you?”)

Buck could not, would not, let Chris down again. He had a thought, a terribly wonderful thought, because home was where the heart is and just because they were spending the holiday at the station, it didn’t mean they couldn’t celebrate it with Christopher.

At the station, surrounded by Christmas cheer, he almost took that first step. He almost gave words to feelings. There was no Abby, there was no Shannon, but everything still felt so fragile, so delicate. His feelings were so big and clumsy and he didn’t want to break this family he had managed to cobble together. Buck didn’t want to risk losing what he had just gotten back. So he didn’t, so he kept his mouth shut and his heart in his chest even though he’d given it away so very long ago.

And then Eddie was buried beneath the ground and Buck tried to dig him out with his bare hands because he could not, would not let this be the reality of things. He wouldn’t survive it, wouldn’t live through it. And then there was Eddie again, magnificent and tired and freezing, and Buck’s heart was beating again.

And then Abby came back, with a fiancé and an apology that was anything but. And then the world went to hell, and he spent weeks with his heart torn in two, with Eddie beside him and Christopher out of reach.

And then there was Ana, there was Eddie taking that step with someone else, moving on from Shannon and giving his heart away. There was Albert nearly dying and Jee-Yun being born, there was Veronica who hated him and Taylor who just wanted to be friends. There were his parents and Daniel and spare parts.

And then Eddie bleeding out after being shot in the middle of the street in broad daylight and it was like the well all over again except worse. It was like the well all over again except Bobby wasn’t there to stop him. Buck dragged and carried and shoved Eddie to safety, kept his heart beating with his bare hands. Eddie’s blood was on his face and on his hands and in his mouth, because trauma had a splash zone and it had never been so literal.

There was Eddie in a hospital bed, and Christopher at home, and Buck with the absolute certainty that it should have been him. Buck with the absolute certainty that it would hurt everyone less if it had been him, because he was the one everyone left, the one everyone moved on from.

There was a man on a crane bleeding out, with no airlift available. There was Bobby, accusing him of acting without thinking, except he had thought. There was the simple equation, of a man in danger and a sniper taking potshots and his friends who all had families at home while his heart had already been taken and given away. There was the simple equation, someone bleeding out, someone trying to kill them, and someone needing to put themselves in danger. There was a simple equation, with a simple answer, because Buck was always spare parts.

And then there was Eddie, awake and ready to go home and blowing all of that certainty out of the water. There was Eddie, trusting him with his heart.

(“Because, Evan…”)

Months went on, where Eddie continued with Ana and Buck tried with Taylor, because while she didn’t have his heart she had at least come back. Months went on, and everything was fine until it wasn’t because Eddie was having panic attacks so bad he thought they were heart attacks.

“Somehow we became a ready-made family,” Eddie had told him after some prodding and poking and badgering. “And I don’t know if I’m ready for that.”

(“You two have an adorable son.”)

“I’ve been Ana,” Buck had told him, ignoring the way Eddie’s words had cracked something deep within him. And perhaps the conversation made him a hypocrite, being with Taylor while his heart was elsewhere, but he knew hers wasn’t in it either.

And then the blackout was over, and Maddie was gone, and Chimney was gone, and Jee-Yun was gone. The blackout was over and everything at work was off. The blackout was over, and so was Eddie’s relationship with Ana. And yet, the distance between him and Buck had never felt so wide. Eddie was still there, except he wasn’t, and Buck was unable to bridge the divide.

And then Taylor was picking her job over him, was breaking his trust to further her career, and while Buck might not love himself as much as he should, he still knew he deserved better.

It ends here, Christmas again. The station is decorated and filled with Christmas cheer that Buck just wasn’t feeling this year. Maddie was still gone, so were Chimney and Jee-Yun. There was still that enforced distance between himself and Eddie and he no longer knew who was keeping it.

It ends here, with the families at the station again because the best of ideas are reusable. It ends here, a holiday song about hearts older than Buck playing on someone’s phone. There’s Eddie and Christopher, clinging to each other and laughing. There’s mistletoe on the wall, hanging above their heads.

(“You two have an adorable son.”)

There’s Buck on the other end of the loft, the distance between them filled with people and ghosts and regrets. There’s Buck on the other end of the loft, and he can’t stop thinking how he regretted not taking his chances that first Christmas. He can’t stop thinking of how now there’s no Abby or Shannon, no Ana or Taylor.

(“There’s nobody in this world I trust with my son more than you.”)

There’s Buck on the other end of the loft, and once he had been afraid of taking the risk because his feelings were too big and everything was too fragile. His feelings had only grown since then, but nothing about this felt fragile or delicate anymore. His feelings had only grown, and at some point he’d stopped being afraid that what they had would shatter beneath the weight of them.

(“Because, Evan…”)

There’s Eddie, standing beneath the mistletoe, and the distance between them looms large but his heart crossed it ages ago.

Buck takes the first step, and then another.
